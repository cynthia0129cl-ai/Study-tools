## Chapter 33 PowerPoint, word for word
Your instructor's slides for this chapter, kept in slide order.

:::slides Slide 1 · What the hematological system is
Structure: hematological system – blood, blood cells, lymph & organs · Plasma (clear yellow, protein- rich) · Solutes (proteins, electrolytes, organic elements)

Function: blood accounts for 7–10% of body weight; average adult has 5–6 L in the circulatory system
:::

:::slides Slide 2 · Where blood is made
Primary site for blood formation and maturation (hematopoiesis) Involved in immune response & makes up 4–5% of total body weight Produces stem cells that can become any type of blood cell: Myeloid – RBC, WBC, platelets · Lymphoid – T or B lymphocytes
:::

:::slides Slide 3 · Erythrocytes & hemoglobin
Erythrocytes – most abundant, small biconcave disk with no nucleus; pick up O₂ from lungs & transport; pick up CO₂ from tissues & deliver to lungs Hemoglobin – oxygen-carrying component of an RBC; contains thousands of Hgb molecules with a pigment (heme) that contains iron
:::

:::slides Slide 4 · Leukocytes & the left shift
Leukocytes – defense against infection; least abundant (4,500–11,000 WBC) – 1% Granulocytes – protect against harmful microorganisms during acute inflammation/infection Neutrophils – fight bacteria & fungi, cell debris, foreign substances; phagocytic; first cell to arrive at a site; remain 6 hours in circulation Eosinophils – destroy invading foreign cells Diagram of left shift: more immature cells are present in the blood than normal
:::

:::slides Slide 5 · Thrombocytes
Thrombocytes – from large cells in the bone marrow · Activate coagulation factors in the blood plasma · Initiate stable clot formation – fibrin
:::

:::slides Slide 6 · The liquid part of blood
Plasma (clear yellow): complex mixture of water, proteins, nutrients, electrolytes, nitrogenous wastes, hormones, and gases · Extracellular fluid · 90% water; 6.5–8% proteins; 2% other molecular substances

Figure: centrifuged blood tube = plasma (55%), buffy coat of WBCs and platelets (< 1%), RBCs (45%). Plasma = water 90%, proteins 8% (albumin, globulin, fibrinogen), other solutes 2% (nutrients, electrolytes, waste products, hormones, gases)
:::

:::slides Slide 7 · The blood filter & RBC life cycle
Spleen: filter · removal of abnormal WBC and platelets

Figure (continued_12): RBC life cycle. Red marrow makes erythrocytes → RBCs circulate 120 days → old/damaged RBCs broken down in spleen → hemoglobin splits into heme and globin → globin becomes amino acids; heme becomes iron (recycled to marrow) and bilirubin (to liver → bile) → nutrients, iron, B12, and folic acid from the intestine return to the marrow
:::

:::slides Slide 8 · Carrying oxygen
Hemoglobin transports oxygen (respiration) · Sufficient & balanced RBCs · Excessive increase in RBCs can raise blood viscosity, decreasing O₂ transport
:::

:::slides Slide 9 · Hemostasis & coagulation pathways
Clotting cascade · Coagulation process: intrinsic pathway, extrinsic pathway

Figure (continued_2): intrinsic pathway (blood vessel injury; XII → XI → IX + VIII → monitored by PTT) and extrinsic pathway (trauma; tissue factor III + VII → monitored by PT) join at factor X → common pathway: prothrombin (II) → thrombin → fibrinogen (I) → fibrin → XIII cross-links → stable fibrin clot
:::

:::slides Slide 10 · Blood's defense role
Inflammation and immunity: eliminate cause of injury · remove damaged tissue · generate new tissue
:::

:::slides Slide 11 · Nutrition, PMH & medications
History – Nutrition: Any recent changes in energy level? Difficulty maintaining daily activities, recreation, sports? Pain, burning, or tingling? Skin color or temperature changes? Swelling (edema)? Bleeding or easy bruising, dizziness, fatigue, or lymph node changes (swelling, pain/tenderness, warmth)? Past medical history: chronic disease – hepatitis, cirrhosis, cancer, clotting factor · Pregnancy – gestational thrombocytopenia Medication history: anticoagulants, aspirin, salicylates, herbs, nutritional supplements, nutraceuticals
:::

:::slides Slide 12 · Head-to-toe hematology exam
Skin, head and neck: color of skin and mucous membranes; skin temperature, capillary refill, edema; palpate skin, lymph nodes; inspect oral mucous membranes and skin; inspect tongue Respiratory: rate, rhythm, depth, sound; do the lungs work harder to increase O₂ delivery? Cardiovascular: vital signs – BP, HR, R, SaO₂ abnormal? Auscultation – murmurs, gallops, irregular rhythms Renal and urinary: urinary meatus for bleeding; urine specimen – hematuria? Musculoskeletal: abdominal pain – hyper/hypoproliferative disorders (sickle cell, anemia, leukemia); bone pain – leukemia (WBC) increases pressure in bone; sickle cell (RBC) – pain, tissue infarctions & swelling; ROM – joint pain, swelling Abdominal: inspection – visible masses, abdominal distention, change in bowel & bladder pattern; palpation – light palpation LUQ

Figure (continued_9): nurse lightly palpating the left upper abdomen for the spleen
:::

:::slides Slide 13 · The CBC
Complete blood count: hemoglobin and hematocrit · red blood cells · white blood cells · platelets
:::

:::slides Slide 14 · Reticulocytes, electrophoresis, LAP, Coombs
Reticulocyte count (retic cells are larger than mature RBCs) – useful in evaluating anemia · Increase – response to hemolysis or blood loss for more RBCs · Decrease – hypoproliferative bone marrow (anemias)

Hemoglobin electrophoresis: separates and measures normal and abnormal Hgb · helps diagnose thalassemias – absent or reduced production of Hgb alpha or beta Leukocyte alkaline phosphatase – elevated from infection, stress, inflammation, pregnancy, steroid use & leukemia · enzyme made by normal mature neutrophils · venipuncture Coombs test: presence of immunoglobulins on RBCs – antibodies · used to diagnose hemolytic disease of the newborn
:::

:::slides Slide 15 · Iron studies
Iron (instruct patient to avoid taking iron supplements at least 24 h) · Serum ferritin – ability to store iron · Transferrin – iron-transporting capacity · Total iron-binding capacity – measures the amount of iron in the plasma – high TIBC = low O₂ saturation
:::

:::slides Slide 16 · PT, INR, PTT, bleeding time, aggregation
Prothrombin time – fibrin to clot · Bleeding time · International Normalized Ratio – oral anticoagulants · Partial thromboplastin time · Platelet aggregation – tests abnormal platelet function
:::

:::slides Slide 17 · Bone marrow aspiration & biopsy
Examination of bone marrow (to diagnose thrombocytopenia, leukemias, granulomas, anemias, primary and metastatic tumors) · Bone marrow aspiration – removal of fluid · Bone marrow biopsy – specimen; soft tissue · Needle biopsy – removal of a core of marrow cells

Figure (continued_8): patient lying on side; needle and syringe inserted through skin and cortical bone into spongy bone and marrow at the posterior iliac crest of the hip bone
:::
