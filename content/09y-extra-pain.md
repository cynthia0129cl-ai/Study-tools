## More from your first study guide
- **Why pain matters:** most universal symptom; 1 of 3 ED chief complaints; 60% of patients report pain at primary care visits; costs $100 billion per year.
- **Acute vs chronic (slide):** acute alerts and prompts withdrawal, avoidance, predictable in course, intensity improves. Chronic: months–years, sustained or spontaneous, disrupts normal activities, unpredictable, inconsistently responsive to treatment.
- **Chronic malignant (cancer) pain:** rapidly dividing cells invade bone/tissue; **<5% become addicted** to opioid therapy. **Breakthrough pain:** bursts on top of controlled pain. Neuropathic: burning, shooting, tingling; **worse at night** (chemo neuropathy, phantom limb).
- **Why two people feel the same injury differently:** prior experience, expectations, anxiety, sociocultural factors, gender, genetics; older adults: mixed evidence, decrease in acute pain.
- **Scales:** numeric 0–10 · verbal best for older adults/limited vision · **FACES for preverbal** · **PAINAD for cognitive impairment.** Self-report = gold standard; **score ≥4 requires a nursing response.** Goal = improve comfort and function, not zero pain.
- **Referred pain:** heart → left chest, left shoulder/arm, jaw · liver and gallbladder → right shoulder/scapula, right upper abdomen · lungs and diaphragm → neck and shoulder · stomach and pancreas → epigastric (pancreas also mid-back) · small intestine, appendix, colon → around umbilicus/lower abdomen · kidneys, ureters, bladder → flank, groin, lower abdomen, low back · ovary → lower abdomen/pelvis.

### Medication table (slide 22: know why we use opioids vs nonopioids)

| Drug | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Nonopioids** | Mild–moderate pain (WHO step 1) and inflammatory pain. **Kept at every step** to potentiate opioids and lower opioid dose. No respiratory depression or addiction. Limits: GI bleed, kidney injury, bleeding (NSAIDs); liver (APAP) |
| **Acetaminophen** | Max 3,000 mg/24 h (1,000 mg/dose); liver toxicity; hidden APAP in Percocet, Vicodin, cold meds |
| **Aspirin** | Inhibits platelet aggregation → bleeding; ↑warfarin effect |
| **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, naproxen, ketorolac) | COX-1 block → GI ulcers/bleeding; kidney injury; ↑bleeding with warfarin; can cause thrombocytopenia. **Ketorolac max 5 days, 40 mg/24 h.** Don't combine with steroids |
| **COX-2 (celecoxib)** | Spares stomach; slow onset (chronic OA); ↑MI/stroke risk |
| **Corticosteroids** | Anti-inflammatory; bone pain, cancer pain adjuvant. Hyperglycemia; taper |
| **Morphine** | Gold standard opioid |
| **Hydromorphone (Dilaudid)** | 5× morphine; good with kidney/liver impairment |
| **Fentanyl** | ~100× morphine; patch for long-term cancer pain (24–72 h to work) |
| **Oxycodone** | Potent, highly addictive |
| **Adjuvants** | Neuropathic: gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, amitriptyline (Elavil); ketamine (Ketalar); lidocaine; clonidine |

- **Mu receptor** = respiratory depression, miosis, euphoria, ↓GI motility. Opioids → start a bowel regimen (laxative + stool softener). Older adults: **start low, go slow; avoid meperidine.**
- **Rubefacients:** menthol ointments, cause local vasodilation, cool sensation. Local anesthetics topical or injected.
- **WHO principles:** analgesics at regular intervals · according to pain intensity · goal: functional quality of life · individualized dosing · evaluate response. **Ladder:** Step 1 mild: nonopioid (APAP, NSAIDs) ± adjuvant · Step 2 moderate: weak opioid (codeine, hydrocodone, oxycodone) + nonopioid ± adjuvant · Step 3 severe: strong opioid (morphine, fentanyl) + nonopioid ± adjuvant. Give at regular intervals, not "on demand"; oral first; PCA = basal + bolus with lockout.
- **Heat/cold:** cold for acute strains and back spasm (20–30 min); heat for back pain without spasm, cramps (5–15 min). Nonpharm: distraction (music, guided imagery, hypnosis, prayer), massage, positioning, TENS, relaxation, deep brain stimulation (also tx for PD tremors).
- **Older adults:** underdiagnosed, undertreated → depression, anxiety, impaired mobility, social isolation, falls, sleep disturbance, malnutrition. Cognitive impairment: self-report, cognition tests, behavior observation.
- **Care planning:** therapeutic partnership; assess pain and functional status; multidisciplinary team (PT, pharmacist, social worker); measure effectiveness (sleep length and quality).
