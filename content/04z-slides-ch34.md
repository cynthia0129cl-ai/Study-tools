Hematology – 4 components: red blood cells · white blood cells · platelets · plasma

:::slides Slide 2 · Epidemiology to complications
Epidemiology: most prevalent nutritional deficiency in the world Risk factors: inadequate iron in the diet · hemorrhage · chronic blood loss · GI problems · socioeconomic – lead paint Pathophysiology: iron deficiency · insufficient hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen Clinical manifestations: hypoxia · fatigue · pallor · tachycardia · tachypnea

Figure (continued_4): glossitis (red, swollen, smooth tongue) and koilonychia (spoon-shaped nails)

Diagnosis: CBC – Hgb & Hct · serum iron tests – ferritin (body's iron stores) < 100 indicates IDA; TIBC; MCV Treatment: increased iron intake – red meat, green leafy vegetables, vitamin C, oranges & grapefruits · iron supplements Complications: heart failure · renal failure · impaired thermoregulation · impaired immune function · psychomotor and cognitive impairment
:::

:::slides Slide 3 · Assess, act, teach, evaluate
Assessment and analysis: signs/symptoms related to decreased oxygenation Nursing diagnoses: inadequate tissue perfusion · fatigue · activity intolerance Assessments: vital signs · serum hemoglobin · fatigue · level of consciousness · blood loss Actions: increase dietary iron · increase intake of vitamin C · minimize blood loss Teaching: dietary sources of iron · signs/symptoms of bleeding · daily iron supplements · dangers of lead exposure · prenatal teaching Evaluating outcomes: adequate iron levels · adequate production of hemoglobin · improved tissue perfusion
:::

:::slides Slide 4 · Too many cells, thick blood
Epidemiology: 5 out of 1 million persons diagnosed annually Pathophysiology: increased production of RBCs · hyperviscosity – thick blood Clinical manifestations: shortness of breath · difficulty breathing when lying flat · headache · dizziness · weakness · splenomegaly · blurred vision Diagnosis: laboratory tests · bone marrow biopsy · genetic testing Treatment: reducing hyperviscosity · preventing hemorrhage Complications: thrombosis · hemorrhage
:::

:::slides Slide 5 · Assess, act, teach, evaluate
Assessment: signs and symptoms related to hyperviscosity, hypervolemia, engorgement of capillaries Nursing diagnoses: ineffective tissue perfusion · activity intolerance · risk for ineffective coping Assessments: shortness of breath · headache · dizziness · generalized pruritus · flushing of the face · fullness in left upper abdomen · fatigue · vision problems · weight loss · lab tests · bone marrow biopsy · vital signs Actions: obtain routine CBCs · increase fluid intake · ensure adequate rest · elevate lower extremities when sitting · modify cardiovascular risk factors Teaching: manifestations of severe thromboembolic events · rationale for aggressive hydration · small, frequent meals · avoid tight clothing · avoid extreme temperatures · bleeding precautions · routine moderate exercise · regular lab tests · genetic testing Outcomes: prevented or delayed complications · prevention of major thromboembolic events · prevention of hemorrhage
:::

:::slides Slide 6 · ITP, hemophilia, DIC & HIT
Epidemiology: 3.8 million patients in the US · Acute – presents rapidly and resolves within a few weeks to 6 months · Chronic – more insidious onset and rarely results in remission Risk factors: malignancy · infection · medications – sulfa · autoimmune conditions – SLE · DIC · African American descent, obese · female-to-male ratio 2:1 Pathophysiology: idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura – following viral infection or immunization in children · hemophilia – deficiency of factor VIII or IX, genetic · heparin-induced thrombocytopenia – immune response to heparin Clinical manifestations: decreased platelets – easy bruising, petechiae, bleeding Diagnosis: CBC with manual differential · coagulation studies · bone marrow biopsy Treatment: resolution of underlying condition · replacement of clotting factors · discontinue heparin Complications: hemorrhage
:::

:::slides Slide 7 · Bleeding precautions
Assessment: signs of low platelets – ecchymosis, petechiae, nosebleeds, black tarry stools, bleeding gums, hematuria Nursing diagnoses: risk of injury · risk for ineffective therapeutic regimen · fear Assessments: platelet count · ecchymosis · petechiae/purpura · frank bleeding Actions: implement bleeding precautions · minimize blood loss · avoid IM injections · avoid rectal temperatures · provide safe environment · minimal BP cuff inflation · minimize blood draws Teaching: bleeding precautions · avoid sexual intercourse when platelets < 50,000 · necessity of frequent lab tests Outcomes: prevention of hemorrhage · minimize risk of bleeding
:::

:::slides Slide 8 · Uncontrolled immature white cells
Epidemiology: acute or chronic · chronic may degenerate into acute phase Risk factors: genetic anomalies · Down syndrome Pathophysiology: unknown stimulus mutates myeloid or lymphoid stem cell · uncontrolled proliferation of leukemic cells (leukocytosis) · apoptosis doesn't occur · bone marrow, spleen, lymph congested with blasts Clinical manifestations: swollen lymph glands · increased infections · anemia · bleeding Diagnosis: lab tests · history and physical · bone marrow biopsy · genetic testing Treatment: chemotherapy · bone marrow transplant Complications: infection · hemorrhage · myelosuppression – result of chemo or radiation · bleeding
:::

:::slides Slide 9 · Neutropenic precautions
Assessment: disease may present as anemia or thrombocytopenia Nursing diagnoses: risk for infection · risk for impaired tissue perfusion · risk for bleeding · risk for compromised family coping Assessments: vital signs – low-grade fever response to infection · fatigue, pallor, dizziness, SOB · excessive bruising, petechiae · labs – CBC, WBC Actions: administer chemo as prescribed · institute neutropenic precautions · prophylactic antibiotics, antivirals, antifungals · symptom management Teaching: neutropenic precautions · manifestations of anemia · manifestations of thrombocytopenia · bleeding precautions · diagnosis of leukemia · adverse reactions of chemo/radiation · possibility of sterility Outcomes: remission · minimize/prevent complications · decreased manifestations of anemia, infection, bleeding
:::

:::slides Slide 10 · Hodgkin's & non-Hodgkin's
Epidemiology: Hodgkin's – 14% of lymphomas · Non-Hodgkin's – 4% of total cancer incidence, 4% of cancer- related deaths Pathophysiology: single progenitor in lymph node develops into solid tumor · spreads to other lymph tissue and spleen · involves other major organs Clinical manifestations: painless swelling of lymph nodes · low-grade fevers · drenching night sweats · unexplained weight loss Diagnosis: H&P · lab testing · radiological testing · lymph node biopsy · bone marrow biopsy · lumbar puncture Treatment: chemotherapy · radiation · surgical removal of tumor · stem cell transplant Surgical management: surgical removal of solid tumor · splenectomy Complications: SVC syndrome · spinal cord compression · hypercalcemia · myelodysplasia · hepatic or renal dysfunction · hyperviscosity · venous thrombotic events
:::

:::slides Slide 11 · Assess, act, teach, evaluate
Assessment: manifestations of systemic B lymphocyte involvement · painless swelling of cervical, axillary, inguinal nodes · low-grade fevers of unknown origin · drenching night sweats · fatigue, anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia Nursing diagnoses: risk for infection · activity intolerance · risk for ineffective coping Assessments: vital signs · pain · neurological status · enlarged lymph nodes · skin · lab tests · lower extremities · activity tolerance · spinal cord & nerve root compression Actions: administer blood products · medications · IV fluids · symptom management · obtain labs · emotional support · primary treatment – chemotherapy Teaching: disease process · risk of infection · maintaining treatment schedules · frequent rest periods · dietary intake · manifestations of complications · potential for sterility Outcomes: optimized quality of life · minimized adverse reactions · no bleeding · no infections
:::

:::slides Slide 12 · Plasma cell cancer
Epidemiology: 13% of hematologic cancers · 5–7 cases per 100,000 per year Risk factors: exposure to herbicides, insecticides, petroleum, heavy metals, plastics, radiation · comorbid plasma cell disease – MGUS, solitary plasmacytoma Pathophysiology: asymptomatic premalignant proliferation of monoclonal plasma cells – less than 10% of bone marrow is made up of abnormal plasma cells Clinical manifestations: fatigue · weakness · bone pain · recurrent infections · weight loss · paresthesia Diagnosis: lab tests · bone marrow aspiration · skeletal survey · bone-density measurement Treatment: bisphosphonates · radiation therapy · DVT prophylaxis · dexamethasone · autologous stem cell transplant · chemotherapy Complications: chemo side effects · anemia · bone marrow failure · bleeding · infection · pathologic fractures · spinal cord compression · hypercalcemia
:::

:::slides Slide 13 · Assess, act, teach, evaluate
Assessment: multisystem effects · bone pain – back · numbness and tingling · anemia · weakness, pallor, fatigue · increased susceptibility to infection Nursing diagnoses: acute/chronic pain · impaired physical mobility · risk for infection · risk for injury · impaired urinary output · fear Assessments: vital signs · fatigue · pain · paresthesias · intake and output · serum and urine calcium · serum BUN Actions: chemo as ordered · bisphosphonate therapy · pain meds · encourage fluid intake as tolerated · position for comfort · encourage ambulation as tolerated Teaching: report fatigue, SOB, dizziness, confusion · report sudden severe pain · report changes in sensation · nonpharmacological pain management Outcomes: pain is controlled · optimal renal function
:::
