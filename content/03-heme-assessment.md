Blood makes up **7–10% of body weight**, and an adult has **5–6 L**. Its jobs: deliver oxygen, form clots, and fight infection through inflammation and immunity.

## Anatomy and physiology
- **Bone marrow** is the main site of blood formation (hematopoiesis), about 4–5% of body weight. Its stem cells become either the **myeloid line** (RBCs, WBCs, platelets) or the **lymphoid line** (T and B lymphocytes).
- **RBCs (erythrocytes)** are the most abundant cells: small biconcave discs with no nucleus. They carry O₂ from lungs to tissues and CO₂ back. They live about **120 days**.
- **Hemoglobin** is the O₂-carrying part of the RBC. Its pigment (heme) contains **iron**.
- **WBCs (leukocytes)** defend against infection. Normal is **4,500–11,000**. Granulocytes protect during acute inflammation. **Neutrophils** fight bacteria and fungi, are phagocytic, and are the **first cells to arrive** at a site. **Eosinophils** destroy foreign cells and respond in allergy.
- **Platelets (thrombocytes)** activate clotting factors and form a stable **fibrin** clot.
- **Plasma** is about 90% water, 6.5–8% protein, and 2% other substances.
- **Spleen (LUQ)** filters blood and removes abnormal WBCs, platelets, and old RBCs.

:::pearl Too many RBCs is also bad
Excess RBCs increase blood viscosity, which decreases O₂ transport. Polycythemia vera works this way.
:::

## Hematologic assessment

**History**
- Any change in energy level? Trouble keeping up daily activities?
- Pain, burning, or tingling? Skin color or temperature changes? Edema?
- Bleeding or easy bruising, dizziness, fatigue, swollen or tender lymph nodes?
- PMH: hepatitis, cirrhosis, cancer, clotting factor problems. Pregnancy can cause gestational thrombocytopenia.
- **Meds:** anticoagulants, aspirin and salicylates, herbs, supplements (goldenseal and green tea can reduce anticoagulant effect). Long-term antibiotics can cause coagulopathy and neutropenia. **Aspirin irreversibly blocks platelets for their whole life span (4–7 days).**
- **Drugs that predispose to bleeding:** aspirin, NSAIDs, heparin and LMWH, warfarin, DOACs, clopidogrel, antineoplastics, penicillins and cephalosporins, sulfonamides, thiazides, and antibiotics (they lower vitamin K).
- **Ginger and garlic promote bleeding** (ask about herbs in the med history).

**Physical exam**
- **Skin and mucosa:** color, temperature, cap refill, edema. Look for petechiae, purpura, bruising.
- **Tongue:** red, swollen, smooth, shiny, tender (**glossitis**) points to IDA or pernicious anemia.
- **Respiratory:** are the lungs working harder to deliver O₂? (RR <12 or >20 is abnormal.)
- **Cardiovascular:** vital signs, murmurs, gallops. Systolic BP is lower in anemia and higher with too many RBCs.
- **Renal:** check the meatus for bleeding and look for hematuria.
- **Musculoskeletal:** bone pain. In leukemia, packed WBCs raise pressure inside the bone. In sickle cell, sickled RBCs cause infarction and swelling.
- **Abdomen: palpate the LUQ LIGHTLY.** *→ why: an enlarged spleen is tender and can RUPTURE with deep palpation.* Stand on the opposite (right) side of the patient for the spleen assessment.
- **Normal lymph nodes should not be palpable nor painful.**

**Tube colors:** CBC uses a **lavender (EDTA)** tube. PT, INR, and PTT use **sodium citrate**. Remove the tourniquet as soon as blood flows (to prevent hemolysis), invert tubes gently, and hold pressure until bleeding stops (longer if on an anticoagulant).

:::note Iron studies emphasized in class (Diagnostic Studies slide 31)
- Avoid iron supplements for at least 24 h before the test.
- **Serum ferritin** = ability to store iron.
- **Transferrin** = iron-transporting capacity.
- **TIBC** measures the amount of iron in the plasma. **High TIBC = low O₂ saturation.**
- Leukocyte alkaline phosphatase: elevated, "thing about the bones" (as written in your notes).
- With hematological disorders, the bone marrow needs to be examined for diagnosis.
- Know reticulocyte count, thalassemia, Coombs, clotting studies, bone marrow aspiration vs biopsy.
:::

## Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy
- **Why:** to diagnose thrombocytopenia, leukemias, anemias, granulomas, and primary or metastatic tumors, to stage Hodgkin's, to evaluate chemo, and to monitor myelosuppression.
- **Aspiration** removes fluid. **Needle biopsy** removes a core of marrow. Usually done together.
- **Most common site: posterior iliac crest**, patient prone or side-lying. Sternum or anterior iliac crest: supine.
- **Before:** informed consent, allergies, bleeding risk (platelets, coags, anticoagulants). Explain they will feel pressure on insertion and a brief pulling pain on aspiration, and may hear crunching. Takes 5–10 minutes. A mild sedative is OK.
- **After:** direct pressure 5–10 minutes and a sterile dressing. Rest several hours. Leave the dressing on ≥24 hours. Report bleeding that soaks the dressing or lasts more than 24 hours. Watch for infection and give analgesia.

## Class-only nursing tips
- **Multiple myeloma:** tingling and burning sensation due to hypercalcemia.
- Patients who get chemo do not drink a lot. **Encourage a lot of fluids beforehand** or they can vomit.
- Most common site of **oral cancer** is the **bottom of the tongue**. **Xerostomia** = dry mouth.

## Case: Marge Starr, 25
Fatigue, SOB, needs afternoon naps. HR 118, BP 90/72, RR 26. Pale skin and nailbeds, red swollen tongue. Labs: RBC 3 million, Hct 31%, Hgb 9, ↓ ferritin. This is **iron deficiency anemia**. The ↑HR and ↑RR are **compensation**, as the heart and lungs work harder to deliver O₂.
