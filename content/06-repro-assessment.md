# Assessment of Reproductive Function

History, the physical exam, and the diagnostic tests you'll see used for the reproductive cancers.

## Key assessment points
- **Gravida** = total number of pregnancies. **Parity (TPAL)** = Term, Preterm, Abortions, Living. Example: one baby at 40 weeks plus one miscarriage = **G2 P1011**.
- **Normal cycle:** 21–35 days, lasting 2–8 days, blood loss 20–80 mL. Ask about the LMP, flow, painful periods, and bleeding between periods or after sex.
- Family history of breast, ovarian, uterine, cervical, or prostate cancer.
- **Speculum exam:** warm and lubricate the speculum, announce each step, and the patient may stop at any time. **Cervical motion tenderness suggests PID.** Change gloves between the vaginal and rectal exam.
- **Breast exam:** inspect sitting with arms in different positions. Palpate the clavicles and axillae for nodes, then palpate supine in light, medium, and firm circles down to the chest wall, including the nipple and areola. Note any discharge.
- **Male exam:** done standing. **Transilluminate scrotal masses in a dark room** (benign masses let light through). Bear down to check for a hernia. **DRE for all men >50** and any man with trouble starting urination or frequency.

## Labs
- **Pap test** samples the **squamocolumnar junction**, where cervical cancer develops.
- **Wet prep:** Trichomonas, Candida, bacterial vaginosis.
- **Hormones:** FSH, LH, estrogen, progesterone, testosterone. **hCG** confirms pregnancy.
- **PSA** is a protein made by the prostate, used as a tumor marker. ↑PSA raises prostate cancer risk 25–60%, but there are false positives AND false negatives, so testing is a shared decision with the provider.
- **Menopause:** estrogen and progesterone ↓, **FSH ↑**. Hot flashes, vaginal atrophy, dyspareunia, ↑CVD risk, osteoporosis. With aging, men have more ED, BPH, and prostate cancer, and all ED and urinary complaints should be investigated.

## Table 64.3 Diagnostic studies: prep and nursing implications ★

| Study | Why / abnormal findings | Prep and nursing |
|---|---|---|
| **Mammography** | Finds tumors <5 mm that can't be felt on exam | **No cream, deodorant, or lotion** on the day. Results take a few days |
| **Ultrasound** | First line for the pelvis (inexpensive). Shows tumors, abscesses, and a thickened endometrial stripe. Guides breast biopsy. Shows scrotal masses | **Full bladder** for a pelvic scan. Explain the transvaginal probe if used |
| **MRI** | Soft tissue: tumors, abscess, bleeding | Remove jewelry. Ask about implanted metal, pregnancy, and claustrophobia. Check allergies before contrast |
| **Colposcopy** | After an abnormal Pap. Magnifies the cervix. Acetic acid and Lugol's iodine are applied, and abnormal tissue does not stain brown. Biopsy is taken | Empty the bladder. **Nothing in the vagina for 24 hours.** Reschedule if menstruating heavily. Pregnancy test, consent. ⚠ **Lugol's is contraindicated with iodine or shellfish allergy** |
| **Laparoscopy** | Ovarian cysts, fibroids, endometriosis (CO₂ distends the abdomen) | NPO the night before, consent, pregnancy test. Rest 2 days. Bloating and shoulder or abdominal pain from CO₂ |
| **Hysteroscopy** | Looks inside the uterus and can remove polyps and fibroids | NPO, consent. Rest 2 days. Nothing in the vagina for 4 weeks |
| **D&C** | Endometrial sampling: molar pregnancy, endometrial cancer | NPO, consent. Normal postop bleeding (count pads). Cramping relieved by NSAIDs |
| **Endometrial biopsy** | Endometrial cancer, endometritis. Done for abnormal uterine bleeding | Pregnancy test, consent. Cramping and spotting afterward (NSAIDs) |
| **Hysterosalpingogram** | Blocked fallopian tubes, uterine abnormalities (x-ray with contrast) | Pregnancy test. Iodine or contrast allergy. Cramping afterward |
