# Immune Function and Disorders
@part: Part 5 · Ch 18–22
@id: immune
@summary: Immune system, immunodeficiencies, hypersensitivity types I–V, anaphylaxis, transfusion reactions, latex, connective tissue disorders, HIV, bioterrorism.

Immune disorders come from either too little immunity (deficiency, which leads to infections) or too much (hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, autoimmunity).

## Ch 18 · Immune function

### 3 lines of defense
- **1st, innate:** physical, mechanical, and chemical barriers (skin, mucosa, coughing, tears, saliva). Nonspecific.
- **2nd, innate:** inflammation. **Histamine** causes vasodilation and ↑capillary permeability, so WBCs move in (chemotaxis) and phagocytize. Immediate and nonspecific, with no memory.
- **3rd, adaptive:** delayed but **specific**, and it has **memory**.
  - **Humoral (B cells):** B cells become plasma cells that make antibodies against extracellular bacteria and viruses. Memory cells remember.
  - **Cell-mediated (T cells):** cell-to-cell contact against intracellular organisms (viruses, fungi), tumors, and transplants. Helper (**CD4**), cytotoxic (**CD8**), and regulatory T cells.
- **Active immunity** comes from infection or a vaccine. **Passive immunity** is preformed antibodies (placenta, IVIG).
- Class: **Agglutination** traps antigens and makes them like a paste. **Opsonization** coats antigens (marks them for phagocytes).

### Organs and immunoglobulins
**Central lymphoid organs** = thymus and bone marrow. B cells mature in the marrow. T cells mature in the thymus, which shrinks with age. **Peripheral organs:** spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, adenoids, Peyer's patches. After splenectomy, high-risk patients may need lifelong antibiotics.

| Ig | Remember it as |
|---|---|
| **IgG** | Most abundant. Crosses the placenta |
| **IgM** | Largest. First antibody made in a primary response |
| **IgA** | In secretions (breast milk, saliva, tears, mucus) |
| **IgE** | Least concentrated. Drives allergy and fights parasites |
| **IgD** | On developing B cells (activation) |

### Assessment (Ch 18)
- **History:** infections (how often, how severe, response to treatment), immunizations, TB test, STIs, surgeries, meds (antibiotics, steroids, immunosuppressants, antineoplastics), nutrition (protein deficiency weakens immunity).
- **Risk factors:** liver, renal, and pulmonary disease, malnutrition, radiation, splenectomy, surgery, trauma.
- **Lymph nodes:** normally not easily felt. Enlarged, tender, or fixed nodes are a concern. **Cancerous nodes are usually LESS tender than infected ones.** Palpate for hepatosplenomegaly. Check joints for swelling and warmth.
- **Labs:** CBC with differential, CRP and ESR, HIV test, cultures, skin tests, bone marrow biopsy, chest x-ray.

### Aging (immunosenescence)
↓T-cell function and antibody response, plus ↑autoantibodies, lead to more infection (pneumonia and flu are top-10 causes of death after 65; shingles), more cancer, and more autoimmune disease. **Infection may present as confusion.** Malnutrition makes it worse.

## Ch 19 · Immune deficiencies

**Primary** = born with it (genetic or congenital), usually found in childhood. **Secondary** = acquired from an outside cause: HIV, chemo (leukopenia), corticosteroids, radiation (pancytopenia), malnutrition, burns, removal of the spleen or thymus. **Secondary is the most common type in adults.** Immune deficiency is the opposite of autoimmune disorder.

:::watch Warning signs of primary immune deficiency
≥8 new infections a year · ≥2 serious sinus infections a year · ≥2 pneumonias a year · ≥2 months on antibiotics with little effect · an infant not gaining weight · recurrent deep abscesses · persistent thrush after age 1 · needing IV antibiotics · ≥2 deep-seated infections · family history.
:::

| Disorder | Patho | Manifestations | Treatment / ⚠ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X-linked agammaglobulinemia (XLA)** · B cell | X-linked recessive, so it shows in males and females are carriers. **BTK gene mutation** means B cells can't mature, so no antibodies are made. About 1 in 250,000 male births | Starts at **6–12 months** (when maternal antibodies wear off). Recurrent **BACTERIAL** infections: ears (most common before diagnosis), lungs, sinuses, skin, conjunctiva, meningitis, sepsis (S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae). Handles short viral infections, but chronic viruses are dangerous | **IVIG** every 3–4 weeks (or weekly subcut). Prophylactic antibiotics, courses twice as long as usual. ⚠ **NO LIVE VACCINES** (5–10% got vaccine-associated polio), so give inactivated polio to the patient and siblings. Older adults on IVIG risk renal failure, fluid overload, clots, hemolysis. Safe food handling and handwashing |
| **CVID** · B cell | B cells normal in number but don't become plasma cells, so antibodies are low | Like XLA but milder. Diagnosed in adulthood (class: **age 15–35**), both sexes. Autoimmune disease, GI symptoms, granulomas, cancers | Same as XLA |
| **Selective IgA deficiency** | **Most common Ig deficiency.** Low IgA with other Igs normal | Often asymptomatic (class: age 14). Recurrent sinus and lung infections. 20–30% have autoimmune disease | Treat infections |
| **DiGeorge syndrome** · T cell | **Chromosome 22 deletion** ("CATCH-22"), autosomal dominant. Pharyngeal pouches don't develop in weeks 6–10, so the thymus is small or absent (no T cells) and the parathyroid is affected | **CATCH:** **C**ardiac (tetralogy of Fallot), **A**bnormal face, **T**hymic aplasia, **C**left palate, **H**ypocalcemia (hypoparathyroidism, which causes tetany and twitching). Opportunistic infections: Candida, viral, fungal. Feeding problems, developmental delays | **Calcium supplements** (prevent tetany, seizures, bronchospasm). IVIG (lasts 3–4 weeks), prophylactic antibiotics, aggressive infection treatment. **Thymus transplant** (ideally by 3–6 months). Bone marrow transplant. ⚠ GVHD from transfused T cells. Monitor calcium and WBC. Class: mother needs genetic testing while pregnant; **FISH testing** detects the chromosome 22 deletion; **puree food** (feeding problems, cleft palate) |
| **Hyper-IgM** · T cell | X-linked. T cells can't signal B cells to switch, so only IgM is made (low IgG and IgA) | Infections in the first 2 years. Pneumocystis jiroveci | Autoimmune blood disorders |
| **SCID** · both T and B | No T- or B-cell function at all (the "boy in the bubble") | Potentially fatal infections | Stem cell transplant |
| **Wiskott–Aldrich** · both | X-linked recessive | Infections, **eczema, thrombocytopenia** (small platelets) | Stem cell transplant |

:::nursing Nursing: any immunodeficiency
- **Assess:** fever, behavior change (irritable, lethargic), appetite, RR and work of breathing, SpO₂, lung sounds, ears, eyes, skin and wounds. Findings may be subtle and nonspecific.
- **Actions:** protective isolation, limit visitors, assign a nurse who is not caring for infectious patients, gamma globulin, prophylactic antibiotics, treat infection promptly.
- **Secondary (therapy-induced):** consider changing or stopping the immunosuppressant if the infection risk outweighs the benefit. Signs of infection: fever, hypotension and tachycardia, cloudy or foul urine, CVA tenderness, rash.
:::

:::nursing Teaching: protect from infection
- Hand washing. Avoid crowds and anyone who is ill.
- **Low-bacteria diet:** no salads, raw fruit or vegetables, undercooked meat, fish, or eggs, or sushi. Safe food handling. Avoid untested well water.
- **No changing cat litter** (toxoplasmosis). **No turtles or reptiles** (Salmonella).
- Report temperature >100°F (37.8°C) (Ch 19 secondary) or ≥100.5°F (38°C) (XLA), cough or URI symptoms, cloudy urine, or a wound that is slow to heal or warm.
- Finish the full course of antibiotics or antivirals.
:::

## Hypersensitivity reactions (overactive immune response) ★

| Type | Mechanism | Antibody / cell | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I · Immediate (allergic)** | IgE on mast cells binds the allergen and the mast cell degranulates, releasing histamine, leukotrienes, and prostaglandins | IgE, mast cells | **Allergic rhinitis (most common)**, asthma, **anaphylaxis (most severe)** |
| **II · Cytotoxic** | IgG or IgM attack antigens on the cell surface, causing complement lysis | IgG, IgM | **Transfusion reaction**, Rh disease of the newborn (erythroblastosis fetalis), **Goodpasture's**, autoimmune ITP, myasthenia gravis |
| **III · Immune complex** | Antigen–antibody complexes lodge in vessel walls, joints, and kidneys, triggering complement and inflammation | IgG, IgM, neutrophils | **SLE, RA**, serum sickness (7–12 days after exposure), Arthus reaction (local, at an injection site) |
| **IV · Delayed (cell-mediated)** | Sensitized T cells release cytokines. Takes **24–72 hours. No antibody** | T cells, macrophages | **Poison ivy** contact dermatitis, **positive TB skin test (Mantoux)**, latex allergy (can also be type I) |
| **V · Stimulated** | Autoantibody keeps a receptor switched ON | Autoantibodies | **Graves' disease** (TSH receptor leads to hyperthyroidism) |

## Anaphylaxis (Type I) · EMERGENCY

:::matters Why it matters
- Lifetime prevalence 1–2%, and up to 1,500 people die of it each year. **Penicillin causes about 400 deaths a year.** Other triggers: foods (peanuts, tree nuts, seafood), insect stings, contrast media, latex, blood. 20% of people are atopic. If both parents have allergies, a child has an 80% chance of allergies.
- **Patho:** the first exposure makes IgE, which attaches to mast cells. The second exposure causes degranulation. **Histamine** causes vasodilation, ↑permeability, bronchoconstriction, and edema. **Leukotrienes** cause bronchoconstriction (wheezing).
:::

:::ss Clinical manifestations ★
- **Skin first:** urticaria (hives), itching, rash, flushing or heat. Angioedema of the lips, mouth, and eyes. Skin signs occur in 90%.
- Dyspnea, wheezing, hoarseness, **STRIDOR** (airway narrowing), crackles
- N/V/D, abdominal cramps
- Anxiety, a **sense of impending doom**, dizziness
- **Anaphylactic shock: HYPOTENSION + TACHYCARDIA** from vasodilation and capillary leak (distributive shock). Circulatory failure means it is progressing.
- **Local (allergic rhinitis):** sneezing, nasal discharge, itchy watery eyes, ↑eosinophils. **Diagnosis by skin scratch test: hold steroids, antihistamines (and NSAIDs per class) for 5 days beforehand.** Results appear in 15–20 minutes.
:::

:::watch Priority actions, in order
1. **STOP the offending agent.** If it's an IV drug: stop the infusion, **change the IV tubing**, and hang normal saline.
2. **IM EPINEPHRINE STAT**, the first-line drug. It dilates the bronchioles, constricts blood vessels (↑BP), and strengthens cardiac contraction. Repeat every 5 minutes, and go to IV if IM isn't working. **Glucagon** if the patient is on beta blockers and not responding to epinephrine.
3. **100% O₂ by non-rebreather.** Raise the head of the bed, unless BP is low.
4. **Second line, only AFTER epinephrine:** diphenhydramine (H1), famotidine (H2), corticosteroids, albuterol, vasopressors, IV fluid bolus.
- Have intubation or tracheostomy equipment ready. Monitor continuously and stay with the patient. Watch for a **rebound reaction** as the drugs wear off.
- Diphenhydramine is sedating (first generation). Second-generation antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine) are for daily use. Steroids: watch for infection risk, ↑glucose, ↑appetite, irritability, impaired skin integrity.
- **Priority assessment: respiratory status.** Nursing diagnosis: risk for ineffective gas exchange.
- **DO NOT COMPROMISE BP** (class): semi-Fowler's if having difficulty breathing; legs up if no breathing issues; do not move people with a hip fracture.
:::

:::nursing Teaching
- Avoid triggers. Know the early signs (rash, itching).
- **EpiPen use is the teaching priority after a bee-sting anaphylaxis.**
- Medic Alert bracelet. Keep the allergy list updated.
:::

### Transfusion reaction (Type II) ★

| Type | Has | Can't receive |
|---|---|---|
| A | A antigen, anti-B | B, AB |
| B | B antigen, anti-A | A, AB |
| AB | Both antigens, no antibodies | **Universal recipient** |
| O | No antigens, anti-A and anti-B | A, B, AB (**universal donor**) |

Incompatible blood causes chills, fever, pain and heat at the site, rash. Agglutination leads to hemoglobinuria, acute renal failure, and then DIC-like bleeding. **Treatment: stop the transfusion.** Plasmapheresis for type II reactions. **Saline is the only compatible solution with blood** (class).

### Latex allergy (Type IV, can be Type I)
- About 2% of people. **High risk:** healthcare workers, multiple surgeries (spina bifida), atopic or hand dermatitis, females.
- Ranges from contact dermatitis on the hands, rhinitis, and conjunctivitis to pharyngeal edema and anaphylaxis (worse with repeat exposure).
- ⚠ **Cross-reacting foods:** avocado, banana, kiwi, chestnut, mango, papaya, passion fruit, tomato, raw potato, peach.
- **Prevention is the best treatment:** vinyl or neoprene gloves, cloth under the BP cuff, keep stethoscope tubing off the skin, don't inject through latex ports.
- **Latex items:** tourniquets, airways, ET tubes, electrode pads, rubber bands, rubber balls, baby bottle nipples. Medic Alert bracelet. Carry an EpiPen.

:::pearl Other type II and III disorders
- **Goodpasture's (type II):** antibodies attack the glomerular and alveolar basement membranes, causing glomerulonephritis plus lung hemorrhage (attacks lungs and kidneys). Treated with steroids, immunosuppressants, and plasmapheresis (± IVIG, dialysis).
- **Serum sickness (type III):** fever, arthralgia, lymphadenopathy, urticaria, nephritis 7–12 days after exposure (1–4 days on re-exposure). Treated by removing the agent, aspirin, antihistamines, ± steroids or epinephrine.
:::

## Ch 20 · Connective tissue disorders
Connective tissue binds the body together (bones, cartilage, tendons, ligaments, blood, lymph). Your notes said to concentrate on **Chapters 19 and 20**.

### Osteoarthritis (OA) · degenerative, "wear and tear"
- **Most common form of arthritis (DJD);** leading cause of chronic disability.
- Hits the **weight-bearing joints:** knees, hips, feet, cervical and lumbar spine, DIP and PIP joints of the hands. Knees bear most of the body's weight.
- **Patho:** articular cartilage and synovial fluid break down → bone is unprotected → **osteophytes (bone spurs)** → narrowed joint space, pain, ↓ROM.
- **Risk:** female, over 55, obesity, prior injury, repetitive-motion jobs and sports.
- **Manifestations:** pain that **worsens with activity and improves with rest**. **Morning stiffness lasting less than 30 minutes.** ↓ROM, bony swelling, deformity, crepitus.
- **Heberden's vs Bouchard's nodes:** "B (more proximal) before H." **Bouchard's = PIP** (middle knuckle); **Heberden's = DIP** (closest to the fingertip).
- **Diagnosis:** history (45+, pain, stiffness under 30 min); x-ray shows joint-space narrowing and spurs.
- **Meds:** acetaminophen (mild to moderate), NSAIDs, intra-articular steroid injections, opioids with caution. **3 g of Tylenol max a day** → check LFTs and kidney function (NSAIDs stress the kidneys).
- **Heat 15 min on and off; cold 20 min on and off. DO NOT put directly on the skin.**
- Weight loss, exercise, PT/OT, assistive devices. Surgery: debridement, synovectomy, fusion, total joint replacement. **90° hip angle after surgery** (don't flex the new hip past 90°).

### Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) · autoimmune, systemic
- Chronic, systemic, autoimmune inflammatory disease. 1% of people; women > men; ages 30–50s. Risks: genetics, smoking.
- **Joint pain is symmetrical; hands are mainly affected.** Synovitis, swelling, erythema, fatigue, **morning stiffness over 30 minutes**, irreversible deformity (swan-neck, boutonnière, ulnar deviation).
- **Dx:** **RF and anti-CCP2** tests; CRP and ESR show inflammation; renal, hepatic, CBC. **Check PPD** (TB test before immunosuppressants).
- Proper nutrition is controversial; class: avoid night(shade) plants d/t water content.
- **METHOTREXATE and DMARDs:** methotrexate is the **first-line DMARD** (others: leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, Xeljanz). Also NSAIDs and glucocorticoids.
- Immunocompromised d/t steroids → report infection. Stay up to date with vaccines. Rest periods, ROM, aerobic exercise, PT/OT. Surgery: joint replacement, fusion.
- **Methotrexate teaching (case study):** weekly dose, no alcohol, **two forms of birth control (teratogenic)**, watch liver enzymes.

### Lupus (SLE) · autoimmune, flares and remission
- Autoimmune, chronic inflammatory disease: **immune complexes deposit in tissues (type III)**. Most common in **African American women of childbearing age**.
- **Triggers:** pregnancy, sunlight, illness, surgery, silica dust, drug allergies.
- No set pattern; flares and remissions. Fatigue, joint pain, rash, photosensitivity, oral and nasal ulcers, alopecia. **Dx: ANA (anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm).**
- **Treatment:** hydroxychloroquine (antimalarial), NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, belimumab. **Sunscreen daily (SPF 50+)**, rest, energy conservation. **No live vaccines**; keep other immunizations current. **Avoid oral contraceptives** (clot risk).
- **Complications:** renal failure, heart and lung disease, clots, stroke, infection.

### Quick reference (Ch 20 slides, not in your notes)

| Disorder | Key patho | Signature signs | Treatment / teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scleroderma** | Excess collagen → hardened, fibrotic skin and organs | Thick skin, Raynaud's, renal crisis (sudden HTN), pulmonary HTN; lung disease is the leading cause of death | Steroids, immunosuppressants, vasodilators, skin care, ROM |
| **Gout** | Uric acid (urate) crystals in joints | Sudden red, hot, swollen single joint (great toe); tophi in chronic gout; kidney stones | Colchicine, indomethacin, steroids; allopurinol for chronic; avoid alcohol (beer), high-purine foods, aspirin |
| **Fibromyalgia** | CNS amplifies normal pain signals | Widespread pain, 11+ of 18 tender points, non-restorative sleep, fatigue, "fibro fog" | Symptom management; labs only rule out other causes |

## Ch 22 · HIV / AIDS
A **retrovirus** that carries genetic RNA and targets **CD4 T cells**. PLWHA = person living with HIV/AIDS.

- **Transmission:** through fluids: blood and blood products, semen, vaginal secretions, mother to child (amniotic fluid, breast milk). **Not casual contact.**
- **Risk factors:** unprotected sex, IV drug use.
- **Prevention:** testing, PrEP, condoms every time, male circumcision, harm reduction (don't share needles).
- **Healthcare worker exposure:** standard precautions; **post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours** (2–3 drugs for 28 days).

### Stages of HIV ★
**Acute infection** (1–2 weeks) → **Seroconversion** (antibodies appear) → **Asymptomatic** (3–10 years) → **Symptomatic (AIDS)** → **Death** (about 2 years).

**CDC staging by CD4:** Stage 1 = 500+ · Stage 2 = 200–499 · **Stage 3 = less than 200 is AIDS.**

### Labs: what you want
- Targets CD4: want **CD4 HIGH, viral load DOWN.**
- **CD4 less than 200 = AIDS.** Prophylaxis for opportunistic infections starts at a CD4 of 200.
- Diagnostics: HIV antibodies, CD4+ count, viral load.

![Slide 21: opportunistic infections by CD4 count](img/s-048-019.png)

### Opportunistic infections by CD4 count (slide 21)
Herpes zoster and TB as CD4 starts to fall · oral candidiasis near 300 · **PCP at 200** · toxoplasmosis, cryptococcosis, MAC, CMV near 100 · cryptosporidiosis, PML below 50.

### Treatment and nursing
- **Antiretroviral therapy (ART)**, Table 22.3 classes. "**-vir**" = antiviral. **Give ART on time; adherence is everything.** Tie doses to a daily routine. Immunizations. With adherence, people with HIV can live a normal life span.
- **Dx:** risk for infection · risk for fluid volume deficit · anxiety · knowledge deficit.
- **Assess:** vital signs and SpO₂, weight trends, CD4, viral load, ART adherence, TB status.
- **Actions:** universal/standard precautions, ART on time, nutrient-dense small frequent meals, emotional support.
- **Teach:** avoid high-risk behaviors, condoms with every encounter, infection control at home, adherence, what to report.
- **Complications:** PCP, Kaposi sarcoma, candida esophagitis, toxoplasmosis, CMV, lymphoma, herpes zoster, IRIS, encephalopathy.

:::watch Report right away (slides)
New cough, increased fatigue, night sweats · fever below 97°F (36°C) or above 102°F (39°C) · new headache, new blurred vision, mental status change · new skin lesions, new diarrhea, weight loss over 10%.
:::

## Bioterrorism
The intentional release of bacteria, viruses, or toxins. An attack is often first noticed as a pattern of unusual illness.

| Category | What it means | Agents |
|---|---|---|
| **A · highest priority** | Easily spread person to person, high death rate, can cause panic, needs special preparedness | **Anthrax · botulism · plague · smallpox (variola) · tularemia · viral hemorrhagic fevers** (Ebola, Marburg, Lassa) |
| **B · second priority** | Moderately easy to spread, moderate illness, low death rate | Staph (enterotoxin B) · viral encephalitis · E. coli (food safety threats with Salmonella, Shigella) · **glanders** (from horses) · **psittacosis** (from birds) · brucellosis · Q fever · ricin · typhus · melioidosis · water threats (cholera) |
| **C · emerging** | Could be engineered for mass spread in the future | Viral encephalitis · novel influenza · Nipah virus · typhus · water threats. *Viral encephalitis is on both the B and C lists* |

:::pearl Anthrax (Category A)
- *Bacillus anthracis* spores; contact with animal products, or mailed spores (2001).
- **Cutaneous:** painless papule → vesicle → **painless black eschar** on the hands, arms, face, neck; 7–14 day incubation.
- **Inhalational:** 1–7 days, then rapid respiratory failure, hemorrhagic mediastinitis, shock. **GI:** from undercooked infected meat.
- **Dx:** culture (blood, sputum, CSF, skin). **Treatment: Cipro and doxy** (ciprofloxacin, doxycycline).
:::

:::pearl Smallpox (Category A)
- Also called **variola**. Eradicated in 1977, but contagious by aerosol, so it's a top threat.
- Lesions on the face, skin, and torso: macule → papule → vesicle → pustule.
- Diagnosis is clinical (PCR only at the CDC). Treatment is supportive (cidofovir shows some activity).
:::

| Other agents | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Plague** | Flea bites; painful swollen lymph node (**bubo**); pneumonic form → ARDS. Tx: **streptomycin** |
| **Tularemia** | Ticks, flies, animals; no person-to-person spread. Tx: **gentamicin** |
| **Q fever** | Cattle, sheep, goats; fever over 104°F. Tx: **doxycycline** |
| **Botulism** | *Clostridium botulinum* toxin, Category A |
| **Glanders** | From horses (*Burkholderia mallei*), Category B. Skin infection; droplets cause pneumonia and sepsis. Tx: **imipenem** |
| **Psittacosis** | From birds (parrots, cockatiels), Category B. "Atypical pneumonia": cough, fever, headache. Tx: macrolide, doxycycline, or cipro |

**Public health response:** detect early (surveillance), confirm the agent in the lab, alert ERs and labs, start prophylaxis, consider quarantine, and keep the public informed.
