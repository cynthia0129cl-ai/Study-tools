:::slides Slide 1 · WHO gets lung cancer
Leading cause of cancer in US for both men and women 2nd most common cancer in both men and women as per ACS statistics, 2022 > 90% caused by smoking · Often asymptomatic until late stage · Most prevalent in persons over age 65
:::

:::slides Slide 2 · How lung cancer develops
Uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells in the lungs; repeated exposure leads to genetic mutations, promoting carcinogenesis 2 major types: Non-small cell – 85% · Small cell or oat-cell cancer – 15%
:::

:::slides Slide 3 · Small cell vs. non-small cell
Small cell or oat cell: represents 15% of LC; grows quickly & spreads to other organs; survival rate only 5–10% in 5 years Non-small cell: represents 85% of LC; 3 types of cell: adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma & large cell; 5-yr survival rate for those diagnosed in early stage is good
:::

:::slides Slide 4 · What causes lung cancer
Smoking (90% of cases) · Second-hand smoke · Radon · Occupational pollutants · Asbestos
:::

:::slides Slide 5 · Signs and symptoms
New cough that does not go away · Changes to chronic cough · Hemoptysis · Shortness of breath · Wheezing · Hoarseness · Chest pain · Headache · Bone pain · Weight loss
:::

:::slides Slide 6 · How lung cancer is diagnosed
Chest x-ray · CT scan of the chest · PET scans · Sputum for cytology · Bronchoscopy and mediastinoscopy · Bone scan and abdominal scans – to look for metastasis
:::

:::slides Slide 7 · Chemo, radiation, pain & palliative care
Nonsurgical: depends on size, location, and stage of tumor · Radiation · Chemotherapy · Pain management
:::

:::slides Slide 8 · Lobectomy, pneumonectomy, wedge
Surgical intervention: lobectomy · pneumonectomy · wedge resection

Figure: three lungs showing a lobectomy, a pneumonectomy, and a wedge resection
:::

:::slides Slide 9 · What to assess and nursing diagnoses
Assessment and analysis: persistent cough · dyspnea · wheezing · hemoptysis · chest pain · frequent pneumonia or bronchitis Problem list Nursing diagnoses: ineffective gas exchange related to disruption of pulmonary cells · anxiety related to inability to breathe effectively · activity intolerance related to fatigue and dyspnea
:::

:::slides Slide 10 · What the nurse does
Provide oxygen · Monitor temperature · Assess breath sounds · Monitor for cough, pain · Position – semi-Fowler · Monitor appetite/weight – provide small, frequent meals with supplements Provide O₂ – increase SpO₂ to ≥ 90% · Position semi-Fowler · Small frequent meals with dietary supplements · Administer meds as ordered: pain medications, antianxiety, bronchodilators
:::

:::slides Slide 11 · After lung surgery & chest tubes
Post-op care, monitor: vital signs · breath sounds · suture line · chest tube – maintain a closed tube system · monitor water-seal chamber · NEVER CLAMP THE CHEST TUBE!
:::

:::slides Slide 12 · Patient education
Teaching: breathing techniques · pace activities · smoking cessation · nutritional needs · medication regimen · pain control Evaluating care outcomes: pain control · sufficient appetite and weight
:::
