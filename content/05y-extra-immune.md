## More from your first study guide

### Ch 18 · Immune function

| | Innate (natural) | Adaptive (acquired) |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | Present at birth | Acquired after exposure |
| **Speed** | Immediate | Delayed |
| **Specific?** | Nonspecific: same response to any invader | Specific to one antigen |
| **Memory** | No | Yes |
| **Lines of defense** | 1st (barriers) + 2nd (inflammation) | 3rd (T cells = cellular, B cells = humoral) |

- **Physical barriers and mechanisms:** intact skin · mucous membranes · protective lining of organs · sloughing, coughing, sneezing, vomiting, urination · chemical: acidic secretions, enzymes in tears and saliva.
- **Acquired immunity:** **active** (your body makes it; lasts years): natural infection or vaccine. **Passive** (borrowed, short): maternal IgG across placenta, IgA in breast milk, immune globulin.
- **Phagocytosis:** neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages engulf and destroy foreign bodies and toxins. Functions of the immune system: remove foreign antigens · phagocytosis · inflammatory response.

| Organ | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Thymus** (central) | In the chest; where T cells mature; shrinks with age |
| **Bone marrow** (central) | T and B cells form; B cells mature here |
| **Spleen** (peripheral) | LUQ; filters old/damaged RBCs; splenectomy → immunocompromised |
| **Lymph nodes, tonsils, adenoids, Peyer's patches** | Peripheral; filter lymph, trap inhaled germs, intestinal immune sensors |

*Review bone anatomy and physiology (class note). Thymic hypoplasia = underdeveloped thymus (DiGeorge).*

| Cell | Job |
|---|---|
| **Neutrophils** (granulocyte) | Phagocytes of early inflammation; destroy bacteria; most numerous |
| **Basophils / mast cells** | Release histamine and heparin; allergic reactions |
| **Eosinophils** | Destroy allergens; fight parasites |
| **Monocytes → macrophages** | Digest microbes; present antigens |
| **B lymphocytes** | Become plasma cells → antibodies (humoral) |
| **T lymphocytes** | CD4 helper (activate B cells and others) · CD8 cytotoxic (kill infected/cancer cells) · suppressor · **NK cells kill cancer cells** |

**Memory:** granulocytes = "**NEB**" (Neutrophils, Eosinophils, Basophils).
**Other slide items:** dendritic cells (macrophage-type, aid T and B cell recognition) · mast cells (skin, respiratory and GI linings; release heparin and histamine early) · **cytokines** (interleukins stimulate leukocyte production and maturation; **interferon** protects against viral and tumor growth; **TNF-alpha**) · **complement system** (proteins that help antibodies destroy microbes; antibody binds pathogen → cascade of enzymes kills it).

### Immunoglobulin memory hooks ★

| Ig | Function | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| **IgA** | In secretions: breast milk, sweat, saliva, mucus, tears | **A = All secretions** |
| **IgD** | B-cell activation | **D = Develop B cells** |

### Inflammation, humoral vs cellular
- **Local (cardinal) signs:** redness · heat · swelling (edema) · pain · drainage (serous or pus) ± loss of function. **Systemic:** fever · chills · malaise · leukocytosis (↑WBC).
- Acute inflammatory response: tissue damage → pathogen invasion → vasodilation (↑blood flow, chemotaxis of leukocytes) + ↑capillary permeability → invaders engulfed and phagocytized + fibrinogen clot border → cardinal signs → systemic response.

| | Humoral | Cellular |
|---|---|---|
| **Cell** | B cells → antibodies | T cells |
| **How** | Circulating antibodies | Direct cell-to-cell destruction |
| **Fights** | Extracellular bacteria, viruses | Intracellular germs, tumors, transplants |

- **Agglutination** = antibodies clump antigens together, "traps and makes like a paste" → easier phagocytosis. **Opsonization** = antigen–antibody complex "coated with paste" → easier phagocytosis.
- **Variables affecting immune function:** age and gender · nutrition and lifestyle · history of infection or immunization · allergies · conditions (autoimmune, cancer, chronic illness, surgery) · medications and transfusions · psychoneuroimmunologic factors.
- **Labs:** WBC 4.5–11.1; left shift = bands = infection; low ANC → reverse isolation. Aging (immunosenescence) → ↑infection, cancer, autoimmunity; **Shingrix at 50+.**
- **Diagnostic studies:** WBC and differential · skin tests; hypersensitivity test · bone marrow aspiration and biopsy · radiological imaging · specific antibody-antigen tests · HIV tests.
- **Case: Grace Hall, 60 (shingles).** 4 days of low-grade fever, malaise, headache, and itching/painful sensation on the right chest; erythematous maculopapular rash with vesicles. **Herpes zoster** = reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus (chickenpox) that can result in **post-herpetic neuralgia.** Increase fluids, avoid immunosuppressed people, report increasing fever or pain, follow up every 1–2 weeks.

### Ch 19 · Immune deficiency extras
- **XLA:** the most common type of primary immune deficiency per the slide (**50% of PID cases**); 1 in 250,000 male newborns. **BTK (Bruton tyrosine kinase)** on the X chromosome. **Hallmark: no circulating B cells or plasma cells; IgG <200 mg/dL.** Each son of a carrier mother has a 50% chance of being affected. First indicator: recurrent bacterial infections of the respiratory tract in childhood. **Diagnosis:** detailed family history · B-lymphocyte surface marker assays · **Western blot** (BTK protein expressed?) · genetic testing · chest/sinus x-ray. **IVIG** = passive immunity (IgG, gammaglobulin) every 3–4 weeks or weekly subQ + low-dose prophylactic antibiotics. **Complications:** pneumonia, empyema, meningitis, sepsis, cellulitis, septic arthritis (60% in children); chronic enterovirus, enteroviral encephalitis, neurologic deterioration. **Nursing diagnosis:** risk for infection related to compromised host defenses secondary to B-cell immunodeficiency. Assess behavior (fever, change in eating, irritability, decreased activity, inconsolable crying in children). Start antibody replacement early → good prognosis. Teach: warning signs to parents, safe food handling and storage, handwashing, avoid contagious people, **seek care for temp ≥100.5°F**, genetic information and support groups. **IVIG = immune globulin (mostly IgG) from human plasma; short-term passive immunity, lasts 3–4 weeks.**
- **CVID:** low circulating B cells, like XLA but appears in adolescents/young adults (**15–35**). **Selective IgA deficiency:** 50% overcome it by age 14; can develop **anaphylactic reactions when IgA is administered.** **IgG subclass deficiency:** sinusitis, otitis media, pneumonia; prophylactic antibiotics, IVIG.
- **Wiskott–Aldrich:** B and T cells affected; X-linked; always the triad: **infection, thrombocytopenia, eczema.** **SCID:** 13 different genetic defects; "boy in the bubble"; cure = stem cell transplant. **CMC (chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis):** thymus and endocrine glands; recurrent Candida of skin, nails, mucous membranes; adrenal cortex hypofunction is the major cause of death.
- **T-cell deficiency:** CD4 helper T cells activate B cells, cytotoxic T cells, NK cells, and macrophages. Deficiency → fungal, viral, protozoan infections (Candida).
- **DiGeorge:** gestational diabetes implicated; pharyngeal pouches, 6–10 weeks. Recurrent infections: yeast, fungal, protozoa, viral (measles, chickenpox, rubella), Candida. **CATCH:** Cardiac (tetralogy of Fallot, murmur) · Abnormal face (low-set ears, wide-set eyes) · Thymic aplasia → infections · Cleft palate · Hypocalcemia. Hypocalcemia: twitching, tingling of lips/around mouth → tetany, seizures, laryngospasm → give calcium, monitor Ca. **Nursing diagnoses:** risk for opportunistic infections · risk for infection · high risk for ineffective airway clearance (laryngospasm related to hypocalcemia). Complications: delayed brain development, speech delays, learning disability, ADHD, autism spectrum, psychiatric and autoimmune disorders, GVHD. Coordinate cardiology, speech, infectious disease. **DiGeorge finding to report immediately: rhinorrhea** (early infection sign in T-cell deficiency).
- **Therapy-induced (secondary):** cytotoxic meds destroy WBCs. **Corticosteroids** keep T cells in marrow → lymphopenia, ↓IgG, suppress cell-mediated immunity. Assess temp >100°F, hypotension, tachycardia, breath sounds, cloudy/foul urine, skin rashes.
- **Autoimmunity** = body launches an immune response against self (SLE, RA, MS, type 1 DM, Graves'). **Hypersensitivity** = overactive immune response.
- **Memory: ACID-S** = Allergic · Cytotoxic · Immune complex · Delayed · Stimulated.
- **Type II epidemiology:** erythroblastosis fetalis (Rh incompatibility; 1 in 1,000 births to Rh-negative women) · Goodpasture's (1 case per million per year) · **myasthenia gravis** (autoantibodies block acetylcholine receptors; 2 per 100,000). Treatment: remove offending drug/blood product, plasmapheresis, corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, IVIG, dialysis, renal transplant.
- **Type III:** SLE (53 per 100,000; women 9× more than men), RA (women 2.5×), serum sickness (allergic reaction to meds with protein), **Arthus reaction** (local vasculitis after intradermal/subQ injection; raised red lesion after 4–10 hours, center ulcer). Treat: remove agent, symptom management, epinephrine or corticosteroids.
- **Type IV:** antibody independent; 24–72 h; sensitized T lymphocytes release cytokines; macrophages cause destruction; range from local dermatitis to anaphylactic shock. Prevention, antihistamines, steroidal creams. **Latex nursing diagnoses:** risk for localized tissue damage · risk for impaired oxygenation. Ask about history of repeated surgeries.
- **Type V:** Graves' disease: antibody binds to the TSH receptor on the thyroid.
- **Most common food allergies:** milk, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, fish, shellfish, wheat, soy, sesame. The latex link is through fruits (latex-fruit syndrome).
- **Type I treatment:** antihistamines/decongestants (diphenhydramine), steroids, beta-agonist bronchodilators. Diagnosis: WBC differential shows ↑eosinophils; scratch test with glucocorticoids, antihistamines, NSAIDs stopped 5 days prior. **Teaching:** identify causes and avoid; signs of initial reaction; EpiPen use; Medic Alert bracelet. **Outcomes:** free of symptoms, normal oxygen, no rash or itching.

### Case: Samuel Smith, 50 (anaphylaxis to penicillin)
Presents with pneumonia and gets **IV penicillin**. Then: short of breath, anxious, itching, dizzy; rapid pulse, blue-tinged lips, rash on chest and arms, wheezing, sense of impending doom. **BP 85/50, HR 130, SpO₂ 89%**. Hoarseness, hives, **angioedema** of lips and mouth.
**What the nurse did:** immediately **discontinued the IV penicillin**, changed the IV tubing, started normal saline; **epinephrine 0.5 mg IM stat**; **100% O₂ by non-rebreather**; cardiac monitor (HR 130 with PVCs); **diphenhydramine 50 mg IV push**; IV fluids; **albuterol** nebulizer for bronchospasm; intubation/tracheotomy equipment ready. Improved: SpO₂ 95%, BP 110/60, HR 90.

### Ch 20/22 · standard additions
- **OA / hip replacement:** don't flex the hip past 90°, no leg crossing, abduction pillow, raised toilet seat.
- **RA:** methotrexate monitoring: LFTs, CBC, folic acid, no alcohol, teratogenic.
- **Lupus (standard):** butterfly rash, photosensitivity (sunscreen), joint pain, lupus nephritis (watch kidneys), hydroxychloroquine (yearly eye exams), steroids. Women 9:1.
- **HIV (standard):** retrovirus (RNA) uses reverse transcriptase to insert into CD4 T-helper cells and destroys them. Normal CD4 ≈500–1,500 cells/mm³. AIDS = CD4 <200 or an AIDS-defining opportunistic infection. ART is lifelong with strict adherence to prevent resistance; goal = undetectable viral load. Not spread by saliva, sweat, tears.

| Opportunistic infection (standard) | Usual CD4 | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP)** | <200 | Dry cough, dyspnea; prophylaxis TMP-SMX (Bactrim) |
| **Oral/esophageal candidiasis** | <200–250 | White plaques; dysphagia |
| **Toxoplasmosis** | <100 | Brain lesions; avoid cat litter, undercooked meat |
| **MAC, CMV retinitis** | <50 | CMV → vision loss |
| **Kaposi sarcoma, TB** | Any | Purple skin lesions; TB at any CD4 |

### Bioterrorism extras
- **Anthrax:** cutaneous = painless sore → black eschar; **inhalation is deadliest.** Not spread person-to-person (standard precautions). Ciprofloxacin or doxycycline.
- **Smallpox:** airborne + contact precautions; lesions start on face/extremities and are **all the same stage** (chickenpox = mixed stages, mostly trunk).
- **Botulism:** toxin → **descending flaccid paralysis** starting with cranial nerves (blurred/double vision, dysphagia, slurred speech) → respiratory failure. Antitoxin, ventilator support. **Not contagious.**
- **CDC categories:** A = highest risk. B = staph enterotoxin B, viral encephalitis, food safety threats (E. coli O157:H7, Salmonella, Shigella), glanders (horses), psittacosis (birds), brucellosis, Q fever, ricin, typhus, cholera. C = emerging (Nipah virus, hantavirus; pandemic flu often listed). **Know category B: staph, viral encephalitis, E. coli. Cat C: viral encephalitis, flu.**
