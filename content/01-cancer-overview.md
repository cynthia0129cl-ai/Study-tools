Cancer is a group of disorders with uncontrolled abnormal cell growth. It is the 2nd most common cause of death in the US (2nd leading cause worldwide, after heart disease). US death rates dropped 32% (1991–2019) because of less smoking, earlier diagnosis, and newer targeted drugs.

## Key terms and pathophysiology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Malignant | Cancerous, unregulated, invasive cell growth |
| Non-malignant | Not cancerous |
| Benign | Not cancerous, does not invade or metastasize, but can become cancerous |
| Carcinogen | An exposure that damages DNA (tobacco, ionizing radiation, benzene, HPV, sun). **The most common risk factor.** Promotes carcinogenesis |
| Carcinogenesis | Initiation and promotion of cancer after carcinogen exposure plus genetic mutations that the immune system misses |
| Apoptosis | Programmed cell death. **Cancer cells skip it** |
| Contact inhibition | Normal cells stop growing when they touch other cells. Cancer cells lack it |
| Dysplasia | Deranged growth (cells vary in size, shape, organization). A **precursor** to cancer but not the same as cancer. No apoptosis results in dysplasia |
| Neoplasia | Uncontrolled proliferation |
| Anaplasia | Cell has lost its expected structure and function (no longer looks or acts like the parent cell) |
| Metastasis | Abnormal cells invade surrounding tissue and gain access to lymph and blood vessels |
| Carcinoma / Squamous cell / Sarcoma | Carcinoma = epithelial cells. Squamous cell = squamous cells. Sarcoma = connective tissue |
| Remission / Palliation | Disease-free state / relieving symptoms when cure is not possible |
| Vesicant | A drug that causes extensive tissue damage if it leaks out of the vein |

:::patho Two hallmarks
**1. Uncontrolled cell growth**
- Lack contact inhibition, so they don't stop growing when they touch other cells.
- No apoptosis (no programmed cell death).
- No anchorage dependence: they grow in atypical places, make their own blood vessels, and survive anoxia and acidosis.

**2. Altered differentiation**: the cell no longer looks or acts like the parent cell. *Well differentiated* cells look like the parent cell. *Poorly differentiated* cells are harder to treat and more aggressive. This is the basis of **grading**.
:::

### How cancer spreads
- **Cell-to-cell (direct invasion).** Example: prostate spreads to the rectum, pelvic floor, lower spine, and hip.
- **Lymphatic.** Travels lymph channels to nodes, where it can be destroyed, grow, lie dormant, or spread further.
- **Hematogenous (blood).** Example: colorectal goes through the portal circulation to the liver.
- A metastatic tumor **keeps the identity of its origin**. Prostate cancer in the rectum is still prostate cancer (a secondary tumor).

### Types and naming
- **Solid tumors** arise from a body organ and grow into masses (e.g., lung).
- **Hematologic ("liquid") malignancies** come from hematopoietic stem cells (myeloid and lymphoid lines, lymph nodes and spleen): **leukemia** (blood cells), **lymphoma** (lymph system), **multiple myeloma** (plasma cells and immunoglobulins).

### Who gets cancer
- Most common cancers worldwide: lung, breast, prostate, colorectal, skin, stomach.
- US: men, prostate > lung > colorectal. Women, breast > lung > colorectal. Children, leukemia > brain.
- 78% of US cancers are diagnosed at age ≥55, partly because immune surveillance weakens with aging.
- Incidence, prevalence, and mortality vary by gender and socioeconomic indicators.

## Risk factors (Table 13.2)
Carcinogens · environment · hormones · genetics · lifestyle · infectious disease · medications (e.g., **beta blockers** as an example from class) · immune status · nutrition. Risks are cumulative.

- **Non-modifiable:** age and genetics. 5–10% of cancers come from known genetic syndromes such as BRCA1/2, p53 (Li-Fraumeni), FAP, and retinoblastoma.
- **Modifiable:** sedentary lifestyle, poor diet, smoking.
- **Red flags for hereditary cancer:** multiple primaries, bilateral tumors in paired organs, younger-than-usual age, a tumor in the sex not usually affected (e.g., male breast cancer), ≥2 first-degree relatives with the same cancer.
- **BRCA1** raises the risk of breast and ovarian cancer. **BRCA2** also raises the risk of pancreatic, prostate, and melanoma.

## Clinical presentation: CAUTION ★

| | |
|---|---|
| **C** | Change in bowel or bladder habits |
| **A** | A sore that does not heal |
| **U** | Unusual bleeding or discharge |
| **T** | Thickening or lump (breast or anywhere) |
| **I** | Indigestion or difficulty swallowing |
| **O** | Obvious change in a wart or mole |
| **N** | Nagging cough or hoarseness |

If a sign lasts **more than 2 weeks**, the patient needs evaluation.

- **Constitutional signs:** vague symptoms, fatigue, fever of unknown cause, unexplained weight loss, night sweats. In lymphoma these are called **"B symptoms"**.
- **Malignancy-specific signs:** usually come from the tumor mass compressing or eroding nearby structures.

## Staging and grading

:::dx TNM staging (exam topic, tables are tested)
- **T** = tumor size · **N** = number of lymph nodes involved · **M** = metastasis (yes or no)
- **Stage I:** small, no obvious spread outside the organ
- **Stage II:** invades deeper tissues or local lymph nodes
- **Stage III:** large or locally advanced, possibly in local nodes
- **Stage IV:** metastasized
- **All hematologic malignancies are stage IV at diagnosis** because they involve the whole body from the start.
- **Grading** describes how differentiated the cells are. Poorly differentiated = more aggressive.
:::

:::note TNM decode (from your class PLQ)
T = size of tumor · N = lymph nodes affected · M = metastasis? yes or no. A patient with **T3, N2, M1** who says "I am relieved that the cancer is still local" needs more teaching: **M1 means it has spread.**
:::

## Diagnosis: goals and tools
**Goals:** find whether a tumor is present and how far it extends, look for metastasis, check how involved and uninvolved organs are working, and get tissue for stage and grade.

- **Labs** such as PSA, CBC, and tumor markers support the diagnosis and are tracked during treatment. **PSA has high sensitivity but low specificity** (false positives, e.g., prostatitis).
- **Imaging:** CT shows mass location and vessel or organ involvement. PET lights up metabolically active "hot spots" (glucose must be controlled before PET). MRI is best for brain, joint, and breast.
- **Biopsy is the MOST SPECIFIC test** and is required for a definitive (cytopathologic) diagnosis. **Excisional** biopsy (whole mass plus margin) is preferred over **incisional** (core) biopsy, which can track cells through normal tissue. **FNA** is less traumatic but yields fewer cells. A **liquid biopsy** is a blood test that looks for tumor DNA. Results take 3–10 days.
- **Endoscopic procedures** (e.g., bronchoscopy) let the provider see inside hollow organs and take tissue.

## Oncologic emergencies
Acute complications that need urgent treatment, grouped as **structural, metabolic, or hematologic**. Slide examples: neutropenic fever, arrhythmia, DVT, hypercalcemia, spinal cord compression. Treat aggressively if the patient can recover. If the emergency signals progressive disease, the team may choose a palliative approach.

Other cancer complications that are not emergencies: anorexia and cachexia, hypercoagulation (DVT prophylaxis is common), effusions, electrolyte and glucose problems, depression. **Constipation in a patient on opioids could also mean bowel obstruction, hypercalcemia, or spinal cord compression.**

## Prevention and screening

| Primary: prevent it | Secondary: find it early | Tertiary: reduce harm |
|---|---|---|
| Risk factor modification, immunization, chemoprevention, avoiding carcinogens. Quitting tobacco for 10 years lowers lung cancer risk 30–50%. PPE for asbestos, daily sunscreen, healthy weight, exercise, plant-based diet, limit alcohol (≤1 drink/day women, ≤2 men). **Immunization:** HPV (cervical, head and neck), hepatitis. **Chemoprevention:** hormone drugs for high-risk patients. Nutraceuticals are not proven | **Screening for early detection:** general check-up, breast, cervical, colon, prostate. A good screening test is **sensitive** (few false negatives) and **specific** (few false positives) | Reduce morbidity and mortality after diagnosis: treat the cancer and manage side effects |

| Cancer | Guideline |
|---|---|
| General | Age ≥20: discuss risk and screening at the annual physical. Share family history |
| Breast | Ages 40–44 may choose an annual mammogram. Ages 45–54: annual. Age ≥55: may switch to every 2 years, and continue while healthy with ≥10 years of life expectancy. Annual clinical breast exam. Ages 20–39: clinical breast exam every 3 years |
| Cervical | Ages 25–65: Pap plus HPV every 5 years, OR Pap every 3 years plus HPV every 5 years. May stop at 65 if prior screens were normal. (See Chapter 65 for the ACOG start-at-21 schedule) |
| Endometrial | At menopause, counsel all patients about the risk and to report any unexpected vaginal bleeding or spotting |
| Colon | Age ≥45: stool test, OR flex sigmoidoscopy every 5 years, OR colonoscopy every 10 years, OR barium enema or CT colonography every 5 years. Stop over age 85 |
| Prostate | Age ≥50 (≥45 with risk factors): talk to the provider about PSA ± DRE. African American men have the highest incidence and 2× the mortality |
| Lung | No general guideline. Yearly low-dose helical CT for high-risk patients: current smokers or those who quit within 15 years, with a 20 pack-year history |

## Treatment: goals and the 4 modalities

:::tx Goals of care
- **Cure:** often defined as 5 years disease-free. Possible with early resectable breast, cervix, colon, testicular, and uterine cancers.
- **Remission:** disease-free, but unclear whether it's gone for good. The usual goal in heme malignancies.
- **Control:** extend life. **Palliation:** relieve symptoms when cure isn't possible.
:::

:::pearl Timing terms ★ (testable)
- **Primary therapy:** the definitive treatment (often surgery).
- **Neoadjuvant** = given **BEFORE** definitive treatment to shrink the tumor (e.g., chemo before mastectomy).
- **Adjuvant** = given **AFTER**, when the patient is disease-free but at risk from leftover microscopic cells (e.g., radiation or oral hormonal therapy after surgery).
:::

### 1 · Surgery
The preferred modality and primary treatment for solid tumors. Goal is total excision plus a margin. Other types: **debulking** (partial removal when the whole tumor can't come out), **redirection of vital functions** (e.g., an ostomy), and **reconstruction**. Cancer patients may come to surgery with chronic pain meds, weight loss and malnutrition, poor skin integrity, organ dysfunction (slower anesthetic clearance), and infection risk. Watch for **phantom limb** after limb surgery.

### 2 · Radiation
Localized ionizing radiation destroys the DNA of cancer cells.
- **External beam** is given in daily "fractions" (5 days on, 2 off). **CyberKnife** is stereotactic: one precise high-dose treatment.
- **Internal = brachytherapy:** radioactive "seeds" placed in a body cavity (cervix, prostate, breast, others). Low-dose seeds stay in for days or are permanent. High-dose sources dwell for a set time and are then removed.
- **Systemic:** radioactive substance given IV (e.g., radioactive iodine for thyroid cancer).

![Brachytherapy: radioactive seeds placed in the prostate near the tumor](img/s-009-005.png)

:::watch Radiation side effects
- Effects match the body area treated. Local injury peaks at **weeks 2–3**. Inflammation comes first, then **fibrosis** (a late effect: adhesions, skin changes, strictures, so a vaginal dilator may be needed).
- **Radiodermatitis:** erythema, pruritus, dry or moist desquamation. Use mild pH-neutral soap. Calendula or hyaluronic acid cream can prevent it. **No rubefacients (Icy Hot, Bengay)** on radiated skin.
- **Fatigue** is very common, builds throughout treatment, peaks at 3–6 weeks. Pace activities and get mild to moderate exercise. Reassure the patient that fatigue does not mean the cancer is spreading.
- **Chest wall radiation:** watch skin, lungs (pneumonitis: dry cough), and heart (late cardiac effects).
- **Nurse safety:** wear PPE. Brachytherapy patients may eliminate radioactive waste in body fluids, so teach the family too.
:::

### 3 · Antineoplastic medications (Table 13.5)
- **Chemotherapy** (alkylating agents, antimetabolites, antitumor antibiotics) destroys DNA. It can be cell-cycle specific or nonspecific. It kills **all rapidly dividing cells**, including hair, skin, GI lining, and bone marrow, which explains its side effects. Many are hazardous drugs. Class example: **methotrexate**.
- **Immunotherapy** (pembrolizumab/Keytruda) boosts immune recognition of the tumor. Assess for allergic reactions. Checkpoint inhibitors can cause immune-mediated adverse effects for up to a year after treatment.
- **Targeted agents** (HER-2 blockers like trastuzumab; Bcr-Abl inhibitors like imatinib; EGFR agents) block a tumor's unique pathway. Many are oral, with drug–food interactions. Check insurance coverage.
- **Hormonal agents** (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, androgen suppression) change the hormone environment that feeds the tumor.
- Regimens combine classes and are given in **cycles**. Delays or dose reductions can compromise the response, so patients should not skip a treatment.

:::watch Immune-mediated adverse effects (checkpoint inhibitors)
Pro-inflammatory effects can hit any organ: myocarditis, diabetes, hypothyroidism, hypopituitarism, colitis and diarrhea (perforation), hepatitis, pneumonitis, nephritis, rashes, fatigue. They can be mistaken for infection, and missing treatment with corticosteroids or infliximab could be fatal. EHRs often flag patients who got these drugs in the past year.
:::

| Adverse effect | Nursing implications |
|---|---|
| **Myelosuppression** ⚠ | CBCs checked frequently. Teach the patient to **call the provider IMMEDIATELY for a fever**. Myelosuppression plus fever is treated aggressively to prevent sepsis and shock. **Neutropenia is ANC <1,500; fever is ≥38°C (100.4°F)** |
| Alopecia | Starts soon after therapy begins and grows back weeks after it ends, often different (curly, thicker, a new color). Address body image, suggest wigs, cover the head in the sun. | Nausea / vomiting | Prophylactic antiemetics. **5-HT3 antagonists are the most effective**; others are NK1 antagonists, steroids, phenothiazines, H1 blockers. **Lorazepam** is for anticipatory N/V. Assess fluid and nutrition status |
| Oral mucositis | Rinse every 2–4 hours and brush with a soft toothbrush. 2% viscous lidocaine rinse for pain. Cryotherapy (ice chips) with 5-FU or melphalan. Palifermin |
| Hemorrhagic cystitis | IV hydration before and after. **Mesna** protects the bladder |
| Hypersensitivity | Premedicate as ordered. Emergency equipment readily available (e.g., paclitaxel) |

| Pulmonary fibrosis | Periodic PFTs (e.g., bleomycin) |
| Hepatic / renal toxicity | Check LFTs regularly. Review renal function before each dose and ask whether the dose needs changing |

| Peripheral neuropathy | Fall precautions: remove scatter rugs and cords, use nonslip bath mats |
| Diarrhea · skin (hand-foot) | Antimotility drugs, fluids. Emollients. Report severe skin toxicity because therapy may need a break |
| Infertility · menopause sx | Offer sperm or egg banking before therapy. Teach about hot flashes, insomnia, fatigue |
| Capillary permeability | Fluid replacement for hypovolemia. Daily weights |

:::watch Vesicants and extravasation
Vesicant = a drug that destroys tissue on contact if it leaks out of the vein.
- Give by IV push or a short infusion. A **central line is ideal**. A peripheral IV should be <24 hours old, in a superficial vein you can watch.
- Check blood return **before, every 2 mL or 2 minutes, and after**.
- **No blood return, pain, or swelling: STOP immediately.** Put no more fluid into that vein. Call the pharmacist. An antidote goes through the catheter and into the tissue if one exists; otherwise use hot or cold compresses per policy. Mark the area with indelible marker, document, and follow up. A plastic surgery consult may be needed.
:::

:::watch Hazardous drug safety
Most antineoplastics are Group 1 hazardous drugs: carcinogenic and teratogenic.
- PPE: impermeable gown plus **TWO pairs of nitrile gloves** (cuffs over the sleeves), a mask, and a face shield if there is a splash risk.
- Use closed-system transfer devices. Dispose of waste in hazardous-drug containers. **Patient excreta are hazardous too**, so teach the family.
:::

### 4 · Cellular therapy: stem cell and bone marrow transplant
Two steps: (1) high-dose chemo ± radiation, which would normally destroy the marrow permanently, then (2) infusion of stem cells or marrow that "rescues" the patient.
- **Autologous** = the patient's own cells (collected and frozen beforehand). **Allogeneic** = from a related or matched donor, with a possible graft-versus-tumor benefit but a risk of **GVHD**.
- Used mostly for heme malignancies and nonmalignant blood or immune disorders. Not routine for solid tumors.
- Other options: CAR T-cell therapy, donor lymphocyte infusion.

## Nursing management (cancer, general)
Cancer care spans every med-surg specialty, across acute and chronic settings. **Pain control is central.**
- **Bleeding risk (platelets <50,000):** monitor the CBC. Keep platelets at least 10,000–20,000 to prevent spontaneous bleeding, and transfuse below that threshold.
- **Infection risk (ANC <1,500):** hand hygiene, no sick visitors, no gardening or soil, no cat litter, flu vaccine, prophylactic antimicrobials.
- **Anorexia:** corticosteroids or megestrol. **Fatigue:** rule out anemia and depression, encourage exercise, conserve energy.
- **Older adults:** frailty, polypharmacy, renal dosing. Infection may show up as acute confusion without a fever. Fall risk.
- Cancer patients should not skip a treatment.

:::pearl Case study: Mrs. Jones
She has a 2 cm immovable mass in the upper outer breast near the axilla. Her mother had ovarian cancer. The plan is chemo first (**neoadjuvant**) to shrink the tumor, then mastectomy, then **adjuvant** radiation and oral hormonal therapy. For **paclitaxel** (a hypersensitivity risk and irritant/vesicant): have emergency equipment ready and check blood return.
:::
