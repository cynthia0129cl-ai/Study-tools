## Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) · NOT cancer

:::matters Why it matters
Affects **50% of men aged 51–60 and 90% of men over 80**, across all races. **BPH is NOT cancer and is not a precursor to it**, although a man can have BPH and prostate cancer at the same time.
:::

:::patho Patho
The walnut-sized prostate surrounds the urethra and starts growing around age 25. Theories: an estrogen/testosterone imbalance, buildup of **DHT (dihydrotestosterone)**, or a diet high in calories, protein, calcium, and polyunsaturated fat. The enlarged gland pinches the urethra and **OBSTRUCTS urine flow.**
:::

![Normal vs enlarged prostate: the enlarged gland narrows the urethra, causing a weak stream and urine retention](img/s-062-027.png)

:::ss Clinical manifestations (LUTS) ★
**Obstructive:** difficulty **STARTING** the stream (even when straining), hesitancy · weak stream · multiple interruptions while urinating · dribbling at the end.
**Bladder changes:** urgency, frequency, **NOCTURIA** (expected finding), the feeling of incomplete emptying, then incontinence and bed-wetting.
**Bladder outlet obstruction:** acute urinary retention, UTI (stasis), bladder stones, bladder damage, hydronephrosis, postrenal AKI, pyelonephritis.
:::

:::dx Diagnosis, and why
- Based on symptoms, confirmed by **DRE** (an enlarged prostate can be felt).
- **Urinalysis** *→ why: rule out infection as the cause.*
- **PSA:** ⚠ PSA can't tell BPH from cancer, because infection and enlargement also raise it.
- **Bladder scan for post-void residual (PVR)** *→ why: shows whether he can empty his bladder.*
:::

:::tx Treatment (stepwise)
- **Watchful waiting / active surveillance** for minimal symptoms and minimal enlargement: a yearly exam with DRE.
  - ⚠ **Avoid tranquilizers and DECONGESTANTS (OTC cold meds)**, which worsen obstruction (they cause constriction and increase urinary retention).
  - Limit fluids in the evening (less nocturia).
- **Medications** help 35–40% of men. Combining the two classes delays progression by up to 67%. Anticholinergic (oxybutynin) if he has an overactive bladder.
- **Catheterization** (intermittent or indwelling) if meds fail and he can't have surgery.
- **Herbal:** saw palmetto, African plum, South African star grass, Cernilton (long-term safety unknown).
- **Surgery:**
  - **TURP is the most common.** A resectoscope through the urethra removes the inner prostate. ⚠ Bleeding and infection. Catheter for 3–5 days. Light activity only.
  - **TUIP:** 2 small cuts, nothing removed (for a small to moderate gland).
  - **Open prostatectomy** for a very large gland. ⚠ Higher risk of incontinence, impotence, and retrograde ejaculation.
  - **Laser:** immediate relief and less bleeding, so it works for patients on anticoagulants.
  - **TUMT** (microwave), **TUNA** (radio waves, outpatient, good when bleeding is a concern), stents (UTIs and pain, not long-term), water thermotherapy, ethanol ablation.
:::

![BPH treatment flowchart: mild symptoms that don't interfere with daily life → watchful waiting. Moderate to severe → meds, surgery, or other treatment. Surgery is usually indicated for retention, kidney damage, overflow incontinence, bladder stones, recurrent UTIs, or severe hematuria](img/s-063-028.png)

### BPH medications ★ (know how they work and what to watch for)

| Class | How it works | Onset | ⚠ Adverse effects / teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha-adrenergic blockers**: tamsulosin (**Flomax**), terazosin, doxazosin, alfuzosin, silodosin | **RELAX the smooth muscle of the prostate and bladder neck**, so the urethra is squeezed less and urine flows more easily. (It does NOT dilate the urethra or lower PSA.) | 2 weeks to 4 months | **Orthostatic/postural hypotension** (rise slowly, fall risk), dizziness, drowsiness, headache, nasal congestion, reflex tachycardia, retrograde or delayed ejaculation, sexual dysfunction |
| **5-alpha reductase inhibitors (5-ARIs)**: finasteride (**Proscar**), dutasteride (Avodart) | **Block testosterone → DHT, so the prostate SHRINKS.** Antiandrogens but don't change circulating testosterone. Cut acute retention by 57–59% and surgery by 36–55% | 3–6 months | ↓libido, ED, ↓ejaculate volume, breast enlargement or tenderness (gynecomastia), rash, orthostatic hypotension, blurred vision. May reduce male-pattern baldness ("hair pill"). **Finasteride is teratogenic: a pregnant person should not handle crushed or broken tablets** *(class only)* |

:::note Class vs book
Your notes say Flomax "dilates the urethra." The book says alpha blockers **relax the smooth muscle of the prostate and bladder neck** and do NOT dilate the urethra. Pick **"relaxes the prostate muscle"** if it is an option. Flomax helps relieve urinary retention and makes urine easier to expel.
:::

:::nursing Nursing, and why
- **Dx:** disturbed sleep (nocturia) · risk for infection (urinary stasis) · increased fall risk
- **Urinary symptoms** *→ why: severity guides treatment.*
- **Temperature** *→ why: fever points to a UTI from stasis.*
- **Focused abdominal exam** *→ why: a distended bladder means he can't empty.*
- Bladder scan (PVR) and urinalysis.
- **Catheterize** *→ why: checks PVR and relieves a distended bladder, protecting it from damage.*
- Give 5-ARIs and/or alpha blockers.
- **Post-op prostatectomy:** manage **continuous bladder irrigation** (removes clots) and give stool softeners.
- **Teaching:** watchful waiting: recognize worsening symptoms and when to come back. Decrease fluids in the evening. **No decongestants or tranquilizers.** ("I'm glad I can still take my decongestants" means more teaching is needed.) Med side effects. Follow-up. Surgical options. Post-op site care. **Report pain, fever, N/V** *→ why: urine stasis from incomplete emptying can cause a UTI.*
- **Outcomes:** less retention, a stronger stream, less nocturia.
:::

## Prostate cancer · slow growing ★ (5 questions)

:::matters Why it matters and risk
- One of the most commonly diagnosed cancers in men worldwide and the **most common in US men**. 1 in 8 men (about 6 in 10 men ≥65). Average age at diagnosis is 66. Rare under 40.
- **African American men** (and Caribbean men of African ancestry) have higher incidence and **2× the mortality.**
- **Risk:** family history (BRCA1/2, Lynch), diet high in **RED MEAT** (heterocyclic amines), high **CALCIUM** intake, high BMI, smoking, chemicals, STIs, more than 7 multivitamins a week.
- **Protective:** vegetables, vitamin E, lycopene, selenium (oysters, tuna, whole wheat, sunflower seeds), antioxidants.
:::

:::patho Patho ★
Slow growing and asymptomatic until advanced.
- ⚠ **Tumors grow on the PERIPHERY of the gland**, so they do NOT obstruct urine flow early and go unnoticed until they cause pain. That is how a patient can have **late-stage disease without ever having trouble urinating.**
- **Metastasizes to:** lymph nodes, **BONE**, rectum, bladder (spreads cell-to-cell to the rectum, pelvic floor, lower spine, and hip). May be curable when localized and responds to treatment even when widespread.
:::

![Normal prostate vs prostate cancer](img/s-065-030.png)
![Gleason scoring: grade 1 is well differentiated (best prognosis, greatest chance of cure). Grade 5 is poorly differentiated (poor prognosis). Two sites are graded and added together, for a maximum of 10](img/s-065-031.png)

:::ss Clinical manifestations ★
- **Early: none.** When it advances, it can look like BPH: trouble urinating, weak stream.
- **Local disease:** asymptomatic, ↑PSA, weak stream, hesitancy, incomplete emptying, frequency, urgency, urge incontinence, UTI.
- **Locally invasive:** hematuria, dysuria, perineal and suprapubic pain, ED, incontinence, loin pain or anuria (ureter obstruction), renal failure, **hemospermia** (blood in semen), rectal symptoms (tenesmus). Bone pain with mets.
:::

:::dx Diagnosis (controversial)
- **PSA** has been the "gold standard" for early detection, but it is overdiagnosing and underdiagnosing, so some providers follow serial PSAs. **PSA ≥4.0 ng/mL means further evaluation** (starts at 4; watchful waiting below). 2.5–4.0 gets an individual risk assessment. <2.5: retest every 2 years. ≥2.5: retest yearly. No PSA value guarantees no cancer.
- **DRE** from age 50 (ACS), or 45 for African American men or a first-degree relative diagnosed before 65, or 40 with more than one early-onset relative. Requires informed, shared decision-making and life expectancy of ≥10 years.
- **Biopsy** (needle, in the office, local anesthetic) after an abnormal DRE or PSA. Then **Gleason score** and TNM.
- Genomic tests (Oncotype DX Prostate, Polaris, ProMark, Decipher) predict growth and spread.
- **TNM stages:** T1 can't be felt · T2 confined to the prostate · T3 locally extensive · T4 fixed to or invading neighboring organs (may spread).
:::

![Stages of prostate cancer (TNM): T1 can't be felt · T2 confined to the prostate · T3 locally extensive · T4 fixed to or invading neighboring organs](img/s-066-032.png)

:::tx Treatment
- **Radiation** is the nonsurgical option. External beam runs 5×/week for 8 weeks. CyberKnife takes 4–5 sessions.
- **Brachytherapy:** radioactive seeds placed permanently through the perineum. Outpatient, for cancer that hasn't spread outside the prostate.
- **Cryotherapy:** liquid nitrogen freezes the prostate. Low success rate, often repeated.
- **Ablative hormone therapy** suppresses testosterone (an androgen that feeds the tumor) when cancer has spread to lymph nodes or bone. It is called **neoadjuvant** when used to shrink the prostate before radiation.
- **Chemotherapy** (docetaxel, cabazitaxel, mitoxantrone, estramustine) once the cancer is hormone-refractory.
- **Radical prostatectomy** removes the prostate and seminal vesicles ± pelvic nodes (open retropubic, laparoscopic or robotic, or perineal). ⚠ **Almost always causes impotence.** The nerve-sparing approach tries to protect the erection nerves.
:::

:::watch Watch
- **Radiation and brachytherapy:** incontinence and/or impotence.
- **Brachytherapy safety:** no sex for 2 weeks, then use a condom to protect the partner from radiation. **If a client is receiving brachytherapy: minimize contact and wear proper (thicker) PPE** *(class only).*
- **Cryotherapy:** impotence, incontinence, rectal complications.
- **Chemo:** hair loss, mouth sores, N/V/D, ↓appetite, infection, bruising, and bleeding, fatigue.
- **Post-prostatectomy:** infection (fever, increasing pain, swelling, bleeding, N/V), clots. Manage bladder irrigation and give stool softeners (no straining).
:::

:::nursing Nursing
- **Dx:** risk for infection · sexual dysfunction · emotional distress
- **Assess:** risk factors · PSA ≥4 ng/mL · DRE: a large palpable prostate plus high PSA needs workup · urinary symptoms (pain comes from obstruction and bladder distention once advanced).
- **Actions:** hormone therapy and chemo · aseptic wound care · bladder irrigation · stool softeners.
- **Teach:** disease process, brachytherapy safety, chemo and radiation effects, signs of infection, prevention (limit calcium, red meat, and high-dose multivitamins; eat selenium-rich foods).
- **Outcomes:** normal sexual function, normal urination, no incontinence.
:::

### BPH vs prostate cancer ★

| | BPH | Prostate cancer |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Benign enlargement, not cancer | Malignant, slow-growing |
| **Location** | Squeezes the urethra (central) | Grows on the periphery, so no early obstruction |
| **Early symptoms** | LUTS early: hesitancy, weak stream, dribbling, nocturia | None early. Late: urinary symptoms plus pain, hematuria, hemospermia, ED, bone pain (mets) |
| **DRE** | Enlarged prostate | Large, palpable, hard prostate |
| **PSA** | May be ↑ (can't distinguish) | ≥4 needs workup. Biopsy confirms |
| **Treatment** | Watchful waiting, alpha blockers, 5-ARIs, TURP or laser | Radiation, brachytherapy, cryotherapy, hormone ablation, chemo, radical prostatectomy |

## Testicular cancer · young men, very curable

:::matters Why it matters and risk
- Not common, but increasing over the last 20 years. About 9,910 cases a year (2022). **Average age 33**, a disease of young to middle-aged men. White men have 4–5× the risk.
- **CRYPTORCHIDISM (undescended testicle) = GREATEST RISK.** One or both testes fail to descend from the abdomen into the scrotum.
- Others: Down syndrome, Klinefelter's, HIV, family history, cancer in the other testicle, smoking, diet, age.
- **Excellent prognosis: 90–100% cure if found early.**
:::

:::patho Patho
- **Seminoma:** slow-growing, men in their 30s–40s, very sensitive to **RADIATION**.
- **Nonseminoma** (embryonal, teratoma, and others): teens to 40s, grows and spreads faster, **NOT responsive to radiation**.
- **Spreads through** (1) the testicular wall into the blood supply or sperm ducts, (2) **lymph nodes to the back of the abdomen (most common)**, or (3) the bloodstream to the brain, lung, and bone.
:::

![Testicular mass in the testis (vas deferens and urethra labeled)](img/s-068-033.png)

:::ss Clinical manifestations ★
- **PAINLESS mass** (the classic sign).
- Some have a dull abdominal ache or a dull ache in the scrotum. A firm or **HEAVY** feeling in the scrotum. Swelling and redness.
- **Mets:** GI symptoms, low back pain, gynecomastia, dyspnea, cough, hemoptysis, a neck mass.
:::

:::dx Diagnosis
- Physical exam. **Self-exam** may find it first and is best done in a **warm shower**.
- **Ultrasound** separates cancer from infection, fluid, torsion, or hernia.
- **Tumor markers: ALPHA-FETOPROTEIN (AFP) and hCG.** They should fall after the tumor is removed. If they don't, look for more tumors.
- **Staging is TNM plus S (serum tumor markers)**, using x-ray and CT.
:::

:::tx Treatment
- **ALL types start with REMOVING the affected testicle (orchiectomy)** and its blood supply through an abdominal incision. This is the **"best treatment"** answer.
- **Seminoma:** surgery, then radiation and/or chemo.
- **Nonseminoma:** surgery plus lymph node removal, then chemo (not radiation).
- **Chemo:** cisplatin, etoposide, bleomycin, ifosfamide, paclitaxel, vinblastine.
:::

:::watch Watch
- **Chemo toxicity:** hair loss, mouth sores, N/V/D, infection, bleeding, kidney damage (monitor creatinine, BUN, GFR), neuropathy, ototoxicity, lung damage (**bleomycin**), hemorrhagic cystitis (**ifosfamide**). Chemo can also cause ↓testosterone and ↑cardiovascular risk (angina, MI).
- **Retroperitoneal lymph node resection (RPLND)** can damage the nerves that control ejaculation.
- **Post-op:** hypotension and tachycardia mean volume loss. Fever means infection.
:::

:::nursing Nursing
- **Dx:** ineffective health maintenance · risk for infection · pain. Assess lumps, swelling, redness, and vital signs.
- **Actions:** aseptic wound care, chemo.
- **Teach:** signs of infection, chemo effects, nerve damage from RPLND, **BANK SPERM BEFORE TREATMENT** to preserve fertility, and **follow up for at least 10 years** (the usual window for recurrence).
- **Outcomes:** early diagnosis, cure, successful family planning.
:::

## Male breast cancer · BRCA2

:::matters Why it matters and risk
- **100 times less common** than female breast cancer (1 in 100,000). Ages 60–70. Non-Hispanic Black men have higher risk, and a first-degree relative doubles it.
- **BRCA2** (more common) or BRCA1 mutation, family history, excess alcohol, testicular disorders, hormone imbalance (↑estrogen from liver disease or Klinefelter's), obesity, gynecomastia, heat or radiation, estrogen therapy (prostate cancer or gender transition).
- *SLIDE vs BOOK:* slide: "2,360 cases in 2014." Book: "1 in 100,000 worldwide."
:::

:::patho Patho and S/S
Uncontrolled growth in the small amount of breast tissue behind the nipple. Because there is so little tissue, it spreads easily to the nipple, skin, and muscle, but the mass is easier to feel. Mostly ductal (DCIS, infiltrating ductal). Lobular is rare.
**S/S:** a lump or swelling, dimpling, nipple discharge, inversion, or bleeding, erythema, skin ulceration, axillary nodes. **Inflammatory type:** red, warm, swollen breast. **Paget's:** tumor grows into the nipple surface.
:::

:::dx Diagnosis
- Clinical breast exam. **Ultrasound if <25 years old. Mammogram if ≥25.** MRI.
- **ER/PR receptor test** tells whether hormone therapy will work. The **HER2 test** tells whether monoclonal antibody therapy will work.
- Sentinel node biopsy, chest x-ray, CT, bone scan, PET for mets.
- **Staging:** 0 in situ · I <2 cm, no nodes · II up to 5 cm · III >5 cm ± nodes · IV mets.
:::

:::tx Treatment
- **Mastectomy is usual** (simple, modified radical, or radical). Lumpectomy is rarely used because there is so little tissue.
- Chemo (adjuvant or neoadjuvant = BEFORE surgery to help complete excision).
- **Hormone therapy only for receptor-POSITIVE tumors:** tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, fulvestrant. ⚠ **Question antiestrogen therapy in a receptor-NEGATIVE patient.**
- Targeted therapy (trastuzumab, pertuzumab) for HER2+: watch for hypertension, bleeding, poor wound healing, clots, kidney damage. Radiation is rarely used.
:::

:::nursing Nursing
- **Dx:** ineffective health maintenance · risk for infection · emotional distress.
- **Post-op:** ↑temperature means infection, ↓BP means bleeding or hypovolemia, ↑HR means bleeding, pain, or anxiety. Check the incision (approximation, minimal serosanguineous drainage) and pain scale.
- **Actions:** meds, change the dressing (aseptic), **empty the drain and record color and amount**, pain meds (helps with cough, deep breathing, and ambulation).
- **Teach:** suture care, signs of infection, breast self-exam (to monitor for recurrence), follow-up, emotional support. Transgender men after top surgery may still need mammograms.
:::

## Testicular trauma · ages 25–40

:::tx Types
- **Blunt (most common):** **sports injuries are the #1 cause**, plus a kick to the groin, MVA, falls, straddle injuries. It can dislocate a testicle. (Class: groin injury in football = blunt trauma.)
- **Penetrating:** stab or gunshot wounds, bites.
- **Degloving / avulsion (least common):** scrotal skin is sheared off by heavy machinery or farm accidents.
- Rupture of the **tunica albuginea** causes bleeding into the tunica vaginalis (a **hematocele**) or a scrotal hematoma.
- *SLIDE vs BOOK:* slide: 85% blunt / 15% penetrating. Book: 80% / 20%.
:::

:::tx Management
- History (mechanism of injury) and **scrotal ultrasound**.
- **Blunt with no rupture: conservative.** NSAIDs, scrotal support (sling or folded towels), ice packs (with a towel), bedrest 24–48 hours. Antibiotics for epididymitis or UTI.
- **Surgical exploration** for internal damage: debridement, irrigation, microsurgery. Orchiectomy is rare.
- **Degloving:** primary closure using the remaining scrotal skin. If there isn't enough skin, temporarily relocate the testis to the thigh (cooler, so sperm production continues) and reconstruct in 4–6 weeks. **Reimplantation within 8 hours if amputated.**
- ⚠ Bleeding and infection are the major risks. Antibiotics before and after surgery. Drains.
:::

:::nursing Nursing
- **Assess:** bleeding, swelling, skin avulsion, pain (usually intense), temperature, wound (redness, warmth, pus).
- **Actions:** conservative care, aseptic wound care, antibiotics, **monitor drain output (excess means bleeding; foul or discolored means infection)**, compassion.
- **Teach:** NSAID dosing, scrotal elevation, ice with a towel, rest, drain care. Call for fever, redness, heaviness, swelling, bleeding, N/V, or worsening pain. Long term: hormone problems, infertility, impotence.
:::

## JP drain and Hemovac: what to know *(class only)*
- Both are **closed-suction surgical drains** that pull fluid from the wound to prevent hematoma and infection.
- **JP (Jackson-Pratt)** = small bulb. **Hemovac** = larger round, spring-loaded canister for more drainage.
- **Suction only works when the device is compressed:** empty it, squeeze the bulb or press the canister flat, then close the plug.
- Empty when half to two-thirds full (or per order) and record the amount, color, and consistency on I&O.
- **Expected:** bloody → serosanguineous → serous. Report a sudden increase, bright red blood, or foul or cloudy drainage.
- Keep it below the wound, secured to the gown, with no kinks; aseptic technique; assess the insertion site.
