## More from your first study guide

### Table 13.2 · risk factors → signs → where it spreads ★ (tables are tested)

| Cancer | Risk factors | Signs and symptoms | Common mets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Breast** | Early menarche, late menopause, nulliparity, late 1st child; BRCA1/2, p53; obesity, high-fat diet | Mass, axillary nodes, asymmetry, nipple discharge | Bone, lung, nodes, liver, brain |
| **Lung** | Tobacco, asbestos, coal/soot/tar, pollution, wood dust | Chronic cough, hemoptysis, dyspnea, chest discomfort, weight loss, paraneoplastic syndromes | Brain, bone, adrenal, other lung |
| **Colon** | Alcohol, polyps, obesity/high-fat diet, Crohn's/UC | Bowel change, ribbon stools, blood in stool, anemia, weight loss | Liver, lung, muscle |
| **Prostate** | Age, family history, pesticides | Pelvic/rectal pain, dysuria, nocturia, ED | Bone, nodes, rectum, bladder |
| **Cervical** | HPV, multiple partners, early sexual contact, tobacco, long-term OCPs | Postcoital bleeding, malodorous discharge, dyspareunia | Endometrium, vagina, pelvic wall, vulva |
| **Ovarian** | BRCA1/2, nulliparity, no breastfeeding, long-term OCPs* | Abdominal fullness, bloating, fatigue | Peritoneum, omentum, pleura |
| **Uterine** | Early menarche/late menopause, estrogen therapy, PCOS, tamoxifen, DM, obesity | Bleeding between cycles, lower abdominal cramping | Nodes, vagina, peritoneum |
| **Bladder** | Tobacco | Hematuria, abdominal discomfort, low-back pain | Nodes, bone, lung, skin, liver |
| **Kidney** | Pesticides, solvents, tobacco | Flank pain, hematuria, weight loss | Nodes, lung, liver, bone, adrenal, brain |
| **Pancreatic** | BRCA2, tobacco, DM, pancreatitis, obesity | N/V, early satiety, vague abdominal pain, jaundice | Liver, peritoneum, lungs, bones |
| **Gastric** | H. pylori, alcohol, ulcers, asbestos, smoking | Indigestion, postprandial fullness, N/V | Nodes, esophagus, liver |
| **Esophageal** | Alcohol, smoking | Dysphagia, reflux, chest discomfort | Liver, lung, bones |
| **Hepatic** | Alcohol, hepatitis A/B/C, Budd-Chiari | Jaundice, easy bruising, N/V | Omentum, peritoneum, pleura |
| **Head and neck** | Alcohol, tobacco, wood dust, HPV | Oral lesions/bleeding, neck masses, jaw pain | Nodes, lung |
| **Brain** | Excessive alcohol | Headaches, personality change, memory/visual deficits | Spinal cord |
| **Leukemia** | Alkylating agents, radiation, genetic mutations | Fatigue, anemia, infections, bruising, petechiae, gum bleeding | Diffuse at diagnosis |
| **Lymphoma** | Chronic immunosuppression, radiation, EBV, HIV | Lymphadenopathy, subcutaneous masses | Diffuse at diagnosis |
| **Multiple myeloma** | Older age, Black race | Fatigue, bone pain, dark infrequent urine | Bone and kidney infiltration |
| **Melanoma/skin** | Sun/UV, ionizing radiation, family | Irregular, new or growing lesions; palms/soles | Nodes, lung, brain, liver, bone |
| **Testicular** | Undescended testes, HIV, age 20–34 | Painless scrotal lump, heavy sensation | Nodes, lung, liver, bone, brain |
| **Thyroid** | Ionizing radiation | Enlarged irregular thyroid, dysphagia | Nodes, lung, bone |
| **Sarcoma** | Arsenic, dioxins, herbicides, radiation | Soft-tissue mass or organ dysfunction | Lung, abdomen, local recurrence |

*Table 13.2 lists long-term OCPs for ovarian; Ch 65 says OCPs are **protective** for ovarian cancer. If asked about ovarian protection, OCPs protect.

**Also:** the 9 risk factors are carcinogens, environment, hormones, genetics, lifestyle, infectious disease, medications, immune status, nutrition. **Carcinogen exposure = #1, but exposure alone rarely causes cancer.** Risks are cumulative. You can't prevent it, so **screen often (secondary prevention)**. Disease-linked risks (IBD → colon, hep C → liver) are modifiable only as far as the disease is managed. Hereditary red flags also include rare histology, and ≥3 relatives in 2 generations. BRCA2 also raises male breast cancer risk. The BRCA1 and BRCA2 test detects breast cancer risk.

### Metastasis: routes and examples

| Route | How | Classic example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cell-to-cell** | Direct invasion of adjacent cells | Prostate → rectum, pelvic floor, spine, hip |
| **Lymphatic** | Through lymph channels of the organ | Breast → axillary nodes |
| **Hematogenous** | Into veins draining the organ | Colon → liver (portal circulation) |

Four steps: **1 primary site → 2 invasion → 3 transport → 4 new growth.** Solid tumors arise from organs and grow into masses that invade and erode tissue. Liquid (hematologic) tumors are all stage IV at diagnosis.

### Stage definitions by cancer (all the size tables in your chapters)

| Cancer | Stage → size / spread |
|---|---|
| **TNM stages** | **0** in situ (mucosa only; colon figure S0 stays in mucosa) · **I** small, no obvious spread outside the organ · **II** deeper tissues or local nodes · **III** large or locally invasive · **IV** metastasized |
| **Breast (female, Ch 65)** | 0 = in situ (DCIS) · IA ≤2 cm, no nodes · IB none or <2 cm + node micromets <2 mm · IIA none/<2 cm with node >2 mm, or 2–5 cm no nodes · IIB 2–5 cm + nodes, or >5 cm no nodes · IIIA >5 cm or any size + 4–8 nodes · IIIB any size to chest wall/skin (inflammatory), up to 9 nodes · IIIC any size to chest wall, 10+ nodes · IV any size + distant mets |
| **Male breast (Table 66.6)** | 0 = DCIS, Paget's of nipple, LCIS · I <2 cm, no nodes · II up to 5 cm ± nodes · III >5 cm, nearby/supraclavicular nodes · IV mets to bone, brain, liver, lungs |
| **Prostate (Table 66.4)** | T1 impalpable, not on US · T2 confined to prostate · T3 locally extensive · T4 invades neighboring organs · N0 none → N1 single node <2 cm → N2 2–5 cm → N3 >5 cm · M1a nonregional nodes / M1b bone / M1c other |
| **Lymphoma (Table 34.4)** | I single node · II ≥2 nodes same side of diaphragm · III nodes on both sides · IV organs + nodes ("E" extranodal, "S" spleen) |
| **Ovarian (FIGO)** | I ovaries · II pelvis · III peritoneal mets or nodes · IV distant |
| **Uterine (FIGO)** | I uterine body · II + cervix · III outside uterus in pelvis · IV bladder/bowel or distant |
| **Prostate grade** | **Gleason:** 2 sites each graded 1–5, added; max 10 (higher = worse) |

**Breast size breakpoints to memorize:** ≤2 cm (stage I), 2–5 cm (stage II), >5 cm (stage IIB/III). Nodes: 4–8 (IIIA), up to 9 (IIIB), 10+ (IIIC).

*Staging* = how big, how far (anatomy). *Grading* = how differentiated the cells look under the microscope (poorly differentiated = higher grade = more aggressive).

### Biopsy procedures ★ (on your exam list)

| Biopsy type | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Excisional** | Removes whole mass + margin. **Preferred** |
| **Incisional (core)** | Cuts into the mass; risk of tracking cancer cells through normal tissue |
| **Fine-needle aspiration (FNA)** | Needle (by feel or imaging) suctions cells near the skin (breast). Less traumatic but fewer cells, limited molecular testing |
| **Core needle** | Wider needle with a cutter; larger sample |
| **Vacuum-assisted** | Imaging-guided probe through small incision; vacuum + rotating cutter; local anesthesia; no sutures |
| **Surgical excision (breast)** | Mass <2.5 cm removed entirely; nonpalpable mass marked with a hooked wire first; sutures |
| **Sentinel node biopsy** | First node the cancer drains to; found with radioactive tracer/blue dye. Determines stage. Negative → no more nodes removed. Positive → axillary dissection (20–40 nodes) |
| **Bone marrow aspiration/biopsy** | For hematologic cancers |
| **Lymph node biopsy** | Confirms lymphoma |
| **Liquid biopsy** | Blood test for cancer cells/tumor DNA (NSCLC, breast, colorectal, GI, ovarian) |
| **Prostate needle biopsy** | Office, local anesthetic, after abnormal DRE/PSA; tissue from 2 sites → Gleason score. Can hurt, bleed, get infected |
| **Colposcopy + cervical biopsy** | Cervix swabbed with acetic acid, viewed with lighted magnifier; abnormal areas biopsied. Cervical biopsy is diagnostic |
| **Endometrial biopsy / D&C** | Uterine cancer: endometrial biopsy (99% sensitive for high grade); D&C scrapes the lining if needed |
| **Ovarian** | Biopsy **NOT recommended** (spreads cells). Definitive = surgical removal of mass + pathology |
| **Bronchoscopy / mediastinoscopy** | Lung: scope through mouth/nose to view and biopsy airways; mediastinoscopy through chest incision checks nodes |

**Imaging and labs:** CT = mass location/organ involvement · PET = metabolically active "hot spots" (needs glucose control) · MRI = brain, joint, breast · contrast shows vessel involvement. Labs are supportive, not definitive. Tumor markers and blood counts track response. **Nurse's role:** explain purpose, prep, post-care, what to expect and when results come.

### Treatment extras
- **Neo = NEW = first.** Neoadjuvant before, adjuvant after (kills microscopic cells).
- **External beam:** machine (CyberKnife = one-time stereotactic). **Patient is NOT radioactive.** **Internal (brachytherapy):** body fluids may be radioactive: PPE, limit exposure. **Systemic:** IV radioactive substance (radioactive iodine).
- **Radiation skin care:** mild pH-neutral soap; **don't wash off markings**; no menthol rubs on radiated skin. Fatigue = most common, peaks weeks 3–6.
- **Targeted:** imatinib (Bcr-Abl, CML), trastuzumab (HER2), ibrutinib, sorafenib. Often oral: teach food/drug interactions. **Hormonal:** breast and prostate cancer.
- **Stem cell transplant:** autologous = self · allogeneic = donor (graft-vs-tumor bonus, risk of GVHD) · matched unrelated · **haploidentical** (half match).
- **Constitutional signs:** in lymphoma, fever + night sweats + weight loss = **B symptoms (fatigue is NOT a B symptom).** Malignancy-specific example: brain tumor → nerve pain and ↑ICP.
- Patients who get chemo do not drink a lot. **Encourage lots of fluids beforehand** or they can vomit.
- **Tumor lysis memory:** cells burst and dump K⁺, phosphate, uric acid (all UP). Calcium binds phosphate, so Ca goes DOWN.

### Table 13.7 · chemo priority nursing problems

| Problem | Cue | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk for infection** | Neutropenia: WBC <1,500; temp ≥38°C | Monitor CBC, hand hygiene, no sick visitors, no gardening/soil, no cat litter, flu vaccine, prophylactic antimicrobials |
| **Risk for bleeding** | Thrombocytopenia: platelets <50,000 | Transfuse below 10–20k; bleeding and fall precautions |
| **N/V** | Nausea, emesis | 5-HT3 (ondansetron) + NK1 + steroids on a schedule; lorazepam for anticipatory |
| **Mucositis** | Oral pain, sores | Soft toothbrush, rinse q2–4h, viscous lidocaine, ice chips with 5-FU |
| **Anorexia/cachexia** | ↓BMI, ↓albumin/prealbumin | Steroids, megestrol, dietary counseling |
| **Alopecia** | Starts soon after therapy | Regrows (may differ); wigs; protect scalp from sun |
| **Hemorrhagic cystitis** | Hematuria | IV hydration; mesna |
| **Cardiotoxicity** | ↓EF (doxorubicin) | Echo/MUGA scans |
| **Peripheral neuropathy** | Numbness, tingling | Fall precautions |
| **Infertility** | | Offer sperm/egg banking before therapy |

**Geriatric:** infection may show as confusion, not fever; renal dosing; fall risk; polypharmacy. **Fever ≥100.4°F (≥38°C) in a chemo patient = call the provider NOW.** Hair loss, dry skin, nausea are expected.

### Screening extras (Table 13.4)
- Breast: 40–44 may start yearly mammogram · 45–54 yearly · ≥55 every 2 years (or yearly) · **self-breast exam NOT recommended.**
- Endometrial: at menopause report any unexpected bleeding/spotting; yearly biopsy if high risk.
- Melanoma: annual skin self-exam. Ovarian, NHL, pancreatic, myeloma: no general screening guideline.
