## More from your first study guide

### White blood cells: the differential ★ ("know neutrophils")

| Cell | % | Job | ↑ / ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophils** | 50–70 | Bacteria, fungi; first to arrive | ↑ bacterial infection · ↓ chemo, radiation |
| **Eosinophils** | 1–3 | Destroy invading foreign cells; allergies, parasites | ↑ allergies, parasites · ↓ steroids |
| **Basophils** | 0.4–1 | Release histamine and heparin | ↑ allergy, inflammation |
| **Lymphocytes** | 20–35 | B cells → antibodies; T cells kill foreign/tumor cells | ↑ viral infections · ↓ HIV, cancer, radiation |
| **Monocytes** | 3–8 | Largest WBC → macrophages | ↑ viral, chronic inflammation |

- **Neutrophils** remain about **6 hours** in circulation; mature = "segs," immature = "bands." ↑ in bacterial infection; ↓ with chemo/radiation → infection risk.
- **Left shift** = immature neutrophils (bands) = active infection. The marrow rushes out immature cells (normal bands 3–5%). Bands aren't very effective at fighting infection.
- WBCs are the least abundant formed element (~1% of blood) but required for life. Normal 4,500–11,000/mm³.
- **WBC differential** = the percentage (proportion) of each type of WBC.

### Plasma proteins and other facts
- **Albumin (60%):** made in the liver; keeps fluid in vessels (osmotic pressure). **Low albumin = problems with nutrition** (also liver disease, burns, hemorrhage) ★ notebook. **Globulins** transport and help clotting; **gamma globulins = antibodies**. **Fibrinogen → fibrin** (clot framework). Plasma = water 90%, proteins 8% (albumin, globulin, fibrinogen), other solutes 2%.
- **Bone marrow** = primary site of hematopoiesis; 4–5% of body weight; **red marrow in flat bones** (sternum, ribs, pelvis, vertebrae). Myeloid → RBCs, WBCs (granulocytes, monocytes), platelets. Lymphoid → T and B lymphocytes.
- **RBCs:** made when the kidneys release **erythropoietin (EPO)**; need iron, B₁₂, folic acid, B6, protein. Each Hgb carries 4 O₂.
- **Platelets:** from **megakaryocytes** (large marrow cells); 25–40% stored in spleen; live 7–10 days; normal 150,000–450,000 (lecture: 150–400k).
- **Spleen:** LUQ "coffee-bean." Filters blood; removes old RBCs and abnormal WBCs and platelets; recycles iron; stores platelets. **Splenomegaly causes:** anemia, PV, leukemia, cirrhosis, mono. Spleen is normally not palpable.
- **Assessment table:** pallor = anemia · petechiae, purpura, bruising = bleeding · glossitis = iron deficiency or pernicious anemia · anemia → ↑HR, ↓ systolic BP, SOB · too many RBCs → ↑BP · hematuria · bone pain.
- Anemia vital signs (notebook ★): **BP↓ HR↑** (and ↑RR). Gestational thrombocytopenia 5–11%.
- Hematology functions: oxygen delivery · clotting cascade · inflammation and immunity (eliminate cause of injury, remove damaged tissue, generate new tissue).

### Clotting: 5 steps, pathways, and factors
**1 Vasospasm** (injured vessel constricts, ↓ flow) → **2 Platelet plug** (platelets stick; need von Willebrand factor) → **3 Clot formation** (cascade → fibrin mesh) → **4 Clot retraction** (platelets contract, pull edges together) → **5 Dissolution** (fibrinolysis removes clot after repair).

| Pathway | Trigger | Factors | Lab | Drug |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intrinsic** (slower) | Vessel injury, factors inside the blood | XII → XI → IX + VIII → X | **PTT / aPTT** | **Heparin** |
| **Extrinsic** (faster) | Tissue trauma; tissue factor (III) | III + VII → X | **PT / INR** | **Warfarin** |
| **Common** | Both meet at factor X | X + V → prothrombin (II) → thrombin → fibrinogen (I) → fibrin; XIII cross-links | | |

- **Memory:** **PITT** = PTT-Intrinsic (heparin). **PET** = PT-Extrinsic-Tissue (warfarin, "War"). **Vitamin K** makes factors II, VII, IX, X. **Calcium (factor IV)** is needed in all pathways.
- **Factors:** I fibrinogen · II prothrombin · III tissue factor (starts extrinsic) · VIII/IX missing in hemophilia A/B (Christmas factor) · XII Hageman (starts intrinsic).
- Notebook ★: Intrinsic → check **PTT**. Extrinsic → check **PT**.

### Lab extras
- A **tourniquet >1 minute** falsely ↑ RBC/Hgb/Hct. **Lavender (EDTA)** tube for CBC; **light blue (citrate)** tube for coag studies, filled completely.
- **ANC ≤500** = severe neutropenia → neutropenic precautions. Platelets: <100k precautions · <50k spontaneous bleed risk · <20k transfuse · <5k fatal CNS/GI bleed.
- **MCHC ↑:** spherocytosis. **Ferritin ↑:** liver disease, inflammation. **Serum iron ↑:** hemochromatosis. **INR ↑** = bleeding risk. **aPTT ↑:** heparin, hemophilia.
- **Coombs** also diagnoses transfusion reaction and autoimmune hemolytic anemia. **LAP:** high neutrophils without high LAP → CML.
- **Ferritin ≤100 indicates IDA** (slide wording). **Transferrin:** low–normal in IDA. **TIBC ↑** and **transferrin saturation ↓** in IDA.
- **Bus analogy:** transferrin is the bus, iron is the passengers. In IDA the bus has lots of empty seats (high TIBC) and few passengers (low saturation). Ferritin is the bus garage, and it's empty.
- **Patient prep for iron studies:** avoid iron supplements ≥24 h before · draw serum iron in the morning (levels drop in the evening) · overnight fast recommended.

### Standard chemistry values (ranges vary slightly by lab)
Sodium 135–145 mEq/L · Potassium 3.5–5.0 mEq/L · Chloride 98–106 mEq/L · Calcium 9.0–10.5 mg/dL (≈8.6–10.2) · Magnesium 1.3–2.1 mEq/L · Phosphorus 3.0–4.5 mg/dL · BUN 10–20 mg/dL · Creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dL · Glucose (fasting) 70–100 mg/dL · Albumin 3.5–5.0 g/dL · Uric acid ≈2.5–7.0 mg/dL. Myeloma hypercalcemia criterion: Ca >11.5 mg/dL; lymphoma hypercalcemia emergency: Ca >11. *(Your class electrolyte table is in the Cheat Sheets part.)*

### Connection checks
Blood is formed mostly in **flat bones** · **EPO** stimulates RBC production · low Hgb → pallor and fatigue · clotting factor deficiency → easy bruising, gum bleeding · inspect mouth/tongue (never deep-palpate the spleen) · differential = proportion of each WBC · thrombocytopenia priority = **prevent injury**.
