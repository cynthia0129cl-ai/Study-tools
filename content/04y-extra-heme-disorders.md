## More from your first study guide

### Iron deficiency anemia
- **Iron-rich foods:** heme iron: red meat, **liver, eggs, clams, oysters**. Nonheme: dark green leafy vegetables (spinach, broccoli, peas), beets, dried beans/legumes, lima beans, nuts, seeds, fortified cereals/breads, Cream of Wheat.
- **Boost absorption:** vitamin C: oranges, grapefruits, citrus juice, **peppers, strawberries, tomatoes**. **Avoid with iron: dairy (blocks absorption), antacids.** Iron is taken with vitamin C **on an empty stomach** for best absorption (your notes). Not with milk.
- Oral iron side effects: nausea, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhea, **dark stools**. IM iron = Z-track (stains skin). IV iron: monitor vital signs.
- **Diet question on the test:** the menu that shows teaching worked is **roast beef, steamed spinach, tomato soup, and orange juice** (heme iron + nonheme iron + vitamin C).
- Greatest IDA risk: a **15-year-old pregnant female** (growth + pregnancy).
- Pregnancy complications: low birth weight, preterm labor.

### Polycythemia vera: the chain
**Too many cells** (blood hyperviscous) → **slow flow** (heart labors; less O₂ to tissues) → **clots** (stasis → thrombosis: stroke, MI, DVT, PE) → **bleeding** (platelets used up/abnormal → nose, gum, GI bleeds).
- **Lecture interventions:** aggressive IV hydration (oral fluids if tolerated) · phlebotomy and monitor Hct/Hgb · oxygen for SOB · reduce stress, provide rest, elevate head · avoid tight clothing, extreme temps · small frequent meals · modify CV risk (stroke prevention) · regular blood tests and genetic testing. **Prioritize with ABCs:** airway/breathing first, then hydration and oxygen based on assessment.
- **Assess:** sudden severe headache = stroke. Watch vitals during phlebotomy. Notebook: high risk for stroke and DVT. Lecture: clots, aneurysm rupture, hemorrhage; engorged capillaries.
- **Hydrate!** Rapid / aggressive IV hydration to dilute the blood (starred).
- **Secondary polycythemia** = from hypoxia (smoking, COPD, altitude) → ↑EPO. **Connection check: smoking raises the risk.**
- Slide epidemiology: 5 out of 1 million persons diagnosed annually.

### Thrombocytopenia: extras
- Affects 3.8 million patients in the US. **#1 side effect = bleeding. Most common medication cause = Bactrim (sulfa).** Higher risk for women (F:M 2:1). Risk factors: malignancy (chemo), infection, medications (sulfa/Bactrim), autoimmune (SLE), DIC, African American descent, obese.
- Diagnosis: **CBC with manual differential** (low platelets), coagulation studies, bone marrow biopsy. Treatment: treat underlying cause · replace clotting factors · discontinue heparin (HIT) · transfuse platelets.
- **ITP:** antibodies destroy platelets; kids after virus/immunization (acute); women 15–40 (chronic). Observe if >100k; steroids, IVIG, splenectomy.
- **Platelet thresholds:** <100,000 start bleeding precautions · <50,000 precautions especially important; avoid intercourse; minimize invasive procedures (lecture) · <20,000 spontaneous bleeding; platelets usually transfused.
- **Bleeding precautions, split:** *The nurse does:* avoid IM injections · no rectal temps, suppositories, enemas · minimal BP cuff inflation (no automatic cuffs) · minimize blood draws/sticks; pressure after every stick · safe, clutter-free environment · transfuse platelets/factors as ordered. *Teach the patient:* soft toothbrush, no flossing · electric razor · shoes when up · daily stool softener, no straining · don't blow nose · no intercourse if platelets <50k · direct pressure ≥5 min for cuts · report bleeding signs · frequent labs needed. Gentle oral care and bowel care to avoid mucosal trauma.

### Leukemia: types and medications ★ (most-tested topic: 5 Qs; notebook says 10)

| Type | Onset | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **AML** | Acute | Myeloid. Linked to radiation, **benzene (oil refinery)**, prior chemo, smoking |
| **ALL** | Acute | Lymphoid. **Most common childhood leukemia** |
| **CML** | Chronic → acute | **Philadelphia chromosome (BCR/ABL) in >90%** |
| **CLL** | Chronic → acute | Lymphoid; can be present for years |

**Process:** 1 Mutation (myeloid or lymphoid stem cell) → 2 Clones (immature "blasts" never mature) → 3 No apoptosis (blasts don't die → leukocytosis) → 4 Crowding (marrow packed → ↓RBCs, ↓platelets, ↓working WBCs). **The WBC count may be high, but the cells are immature and useless → infections + anemia + bleeding.** Early on WBC may look normal. All types are fatal if untreated. Chronic can degenerate into an acute phase.

| Treatment | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Chemotherapy** (primary) | Phases: induction (get remission) → consolidation (kill hidden disease) → maintenance (prevent relapse). IV, intrathecal, oral. **AML example: daunorubicin + cytarabine (Ara-C)** |
| **Targeted therapy** | **Imatinib** (Bcr-Abl → CML); **ibrutinib** (CLL) |
| **Immunotherapy** | Monoclonal antibodies; CAR-T (patient's T cells engineered to kill leukemia) |
| **Prophylactic antimicrobials** | Antibiotics (levofloxacin/moxifloxacin), antivirals (acyclovir), antifungals (fluconazole/Diflucan) |
| **Growth factor** | **Filgrastim (G-CSF)** ↑ neutrophils; bone pain side effect. Textbook: not generally indicated in leukemia (could fuel it), but the case patient got it |
| **Blood products** | PRBCs, platelets (supportive care) |
| **Tumor lysis prevention** | IV fluids, allopurinol (prevent), rasburicase |
| **Bone marrow / stem cell transplant** | Refractory or relapsed disease. Risk: graft-versus-host disease |

**Remission** = no detectable leukemia cells in marrow (not a cure). Lecture: remission possible, but recurrence risk when immune suppression occurs.
**Clinical manifestations explained:** swollen lymph glands/LUQ pain = blasts congest nodes and spleen · increased infections, low-grade fever = few mature neutrophils · anemia = RBCs crowded out · bleeding = thrombocytopenia · weight loss, bone pain = high metabolism and swollen marrow ↑ pressure in bone. Diagnosis includes CBC, **peripheral smear**, H&P, bone marrow biopsy, genetic testing, flow cytometry. Complications: infection (top cause of death outside disease progression), hemorrhage, myelosuppression.
**Neutropenic precautions (notebook ★):** no humidifiers · no plants · **reverse isolation (YOU wear PPE) to protect the patient.** Staff/visitors wear PPE when required; restrict visitors with communicable diseases.
**Connection check:** which result raises infection risk? **B. ANC 400/mm³.**

### Lymphoma: extras

| | Hodgkin | Non-Hodgkin |
|---|---|---|
| **Hallmark** | **Reed–Sternberg cells** | Dozens of B- and T-cell subtypes |
| **Frequency** | 14% of lymphomas; better survival | 4% of all cancers and cancer deaths |
| **Ages** | Childhood–young adult, then after 55 | Preadolescence, late adulthood |
| **Risk** | Epstein–Barr virus; first-degree relative | Pesticides, autoimmune, transplant immunosuppression, HIV, EBV, H. pylori |

Lymphoma complications: SVC syndrome (edema of face and arms, SOB, chest pain, dysphagia; lecture: tachycardia, stress) · spinal cord compression (neuro deficits, paralysis → immediate radiation/chemo) · hypercalcemia (Ca >11) · **myelodysplasia** (anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia) · hepatic/renal dysfunction, hyperviscosity, DVT. **Exam focus: Hodgkin complications and symptoms.**

### Multiple myeloma: extras ★ (exam: nursing interventions)
- **Hypercalcemia signs:** tingling and burning sensation (your notes) · confusion, lethargy · muscle weakness · polyuria, dehydration · constipation, nausea · bradycardia/heart block · kidney stones.
- Risk: oil refinery (petroleum), average age 62, men, 2× in people of African descent. Also dark, infrequent urine.
- Bisphosphonates slow bone breakdown. Notes: **"Boniva" = ibandronate**; also pamidronate, zoledronate.
- Teach: report fatigue, SOB, dizziness, confusion · sudden severe pain (fracture) · new numbness/tingling (cord compression) · use nonpharm pain management (**guided imagery**) · **energy conservation.**
- Lecture: more common in men; nursing = pain management (pharm + nonpharm), high fluid intake to prevent stones and clear calcium, positioning, safe ambulation and weight bearing, monitor Ca/BUN/creatinine.

### Heme disorders at a glance (lecture summary)

| Disorder | Key tests / diagnosis | Primary nursing actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron-deficiency anemia** | CBC (↓Hgb, ↓MCV), ↓ferritin, ↑TIBC | Iron + vitamin C diet, supplements, find bleeding |
| **Polycythemia vera** | CBC (↑RBC), bone marrow biopsy, genetic testing | Hydration, phlebotomy, oxygen, elevate head, avoid tight clothing |
| **Thrombocytopenia** | CBC (↓platelets), coag studies, bone marrow biopsy | Bleeding precautions, no IM injections, minimize draws, platelet transfusion |
| **Leukemia** | CBC, bone marrow biopsy, genetic testing | Chemo, neutropenic precautions, infection monitoring |
| **Lymphoma** | Imaging, lymph node biopsy, bone marrow biopsy | Symptom management, chemo/radiation, watch cord compression/SVC, emotional support |
| **Multiple myeloma** | CBC, serum/urine proteins, bone marrow biopsy, skeletal survey | Pain control, bisphosphonates, fluids, monitor Ca/BUN/creatinine |

**Lecture key terms:** hyperviscosity = thick blood from ↑ cells · splenomegaly = enlarged spleen · myelosuppression = marrow suppression → cytopenias · neutropenic precautions = protect immunocompromised from infection · bleeding precautions = minimize bleeding with thrombocytopenia · hypercalcemia = high serum Ca from bone breakdown.
