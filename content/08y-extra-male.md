## More from your first study guide
- **BPH drug classes:** alpha blockers ("-osin") and 5-ARIs ("-steride"). **5-ARIs also ↓PSA** and shrink the gland over 3–6 months. Anticholinergic: oxybutynin for overactive bladder.
- **Screening talk starts at:** 50 (average risk; DRE age 50 per ACS) · 45 (African American or first-degree relative diagnosed <65) · 40 (more than one first-degree relative at an early age). PSA ≥4 → further assessment (your notes: starts at 4 for watchful waiting); <2.5 retest every 2 years.
- **Prostate post-op complications ★ (exam):** impotence/ED (most expected after radical prostatectomy) · urinary incontinence · infection (fever, ↑pain, swelling, redness, drainage, N/V) · bleeding/hemorrhage and clots in urine (**bladder irrigation clears clots**) · retrograde ejaculation (TURP/open prostatectomy) · rectal complications (cryotherapy) · sexual dysfunction and emotional distress (nursing dx).
- Also standard after prostatectomy/TURP: continuous bladder irrigation keeps urine light pink, catheter care/traction, bladder spasms (antispasmodics), Kegel exercises for incontinence, early ambulation for DVT prevention.
- **Prostate cancer:** good 5-year survival; PSA is the "preferred standard" test. Brachytherapy: minimize contact and wear thicker PPE; no sex 2 weeks, then condoms. Chemo when hormone-refractory (docetaxel).
- **Testicular:** average age 33 (20–34 per Table 13.2). **Cure rate 90–100% if early; 95% overall, 80% with mets.** Chemo: cisplatin, etoposide, bleomycin (watch kidneys, lungs, hearing). Teach testicular self-exam in a warm shower.
- **Male breast cancer meds:** tamoxifen in men: ED, headaches, hot flashes. Neoadjuvant = before (shrink); adjuvant = after (insurance vs recurrence).
- **Slide wording, Ch 66:** testicular nursing diagnoses: ineffective health maintenance, risk for infection. Cryptorchidism = incomplete descent of one or both testes from the abdomen into the inguinal canal.

### Case: Scott Andrews, 65 (BPH)
Difficulty urinating, **pain with urination, occasional blood-tinged urine**, urgency, **nocturia 3–4 times a night**, occasional incontinence. Plan: PSA, urinalysis, DRE. DRE result, **PSA <2 ng/mL**, normal urinalysis → best intervention is **watchful waiting.** If symptoms worsen: 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors or alpha-adrenergic blockers. Nurse teaches watchful waiting and the importance of follow-up.
Answers: why BPH causes symptoms = obstruction of urine flow · why watchful waiting = minimal symptoms and enlargement · needs more teaching = "Glad I can take my decongestants" · Flomax = relaxes the prostate muscle · report pain, fever, N/V = urine stasis can cause a UTI.
