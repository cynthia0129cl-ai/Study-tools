# Slides ch13

## Chapter 13 PowerPoint, word for word
Your instructor's slides for this chapter, kept in slide order.

:::slides Slide 1 · Key definitions
Cancer – group of disorders characterized by uncontrolled abnormal cell growth Malignant cells – are characteristic of cancers Non-malignant – non cancerous Benign – not cancerous, but can become cancerous Carcinogens – promotes carcinogenesis Metastasis – abnormal cells that invade the surrounding tissue & gain access to lymph and blood vessels
:::

:::slides Slide 2 · How a normal cell becomes cancer
Carcinogenesis – multiple carcinogen exposures & genetic mutations Uncontrolled cell growth – no apoptosis, results in dysplasia – can be precursor to cancer Altered cell differentiation – do not look like the parent cell Metastasis – spread of tumors
:::

:::slides Slide 3 · WHO gets cancer
Second leading cause of death worldwide · Expected to increase over time Most common types globally: lung, breast, prostate, colorectal, skin, stomach Incidence, prevalence & mortality vary on gender, socioeconomic indicators; carcinogen exposure – most common risk factor
:::

:::slides Slide 4 · What raises cancer risk
Carcinogens · Environment · Hormones · Genetics · Lifestyle · Infectious disease · Medications · Immune status · Nutrition · Table 13.2 – common cancers with risk factors
:::

:::slides Slide 5 · Solid vs. hematological
Solid tumor malignancies – arise from specific body organs, grow into masses Hematological malignancies – arise from hematopoietic cells; give rise to myeloid & lymphoid cells (lymph nodes, spleen)
:::

:::slides Slide 6 · TNM and stages I–IV
Staging determines the size of the tumor, the lymph node involvement & metastasis Tumor, nodes, metastasis (TNM): T – tumor size · N – number of lymph nodes involved · M – presence of metastasis Stages 1 to 4
:::

:::slides Slide 7 · CAUTION, constitutional & specific signs
CAUTION symptoms · Constitutional signs – vague symptoms, fatigue, fever of unknown etiology, unexplained weight loss, night sweats · Malignancy-specific signs & symptoms – often related to tumor- mass compression or erosion
:::

:::slides Slide 8 · The ones that can kill fast
Acute clinical complications – require urgent treatment secondary to tumor growth Categorized as structural, metabolic, or hematological Organ dysfunction may be present Common ex: neutropenic fever, arrhythmia, DVT, hypercalcemia, spinal cord compression
:::

:::slides Slide 9 · Primary, secondary, tertiary
Primary: risk factor modification, immunization, chemoprevention, avoiding carcinogens Secondary: screening for early detection – general check-up, breast, cervical, colon, prostate Tertiary: reducing morbidity and mortality once disease is diagnosed
:::

:::slides Slide 10 · Goals and diagnostic tests
Determine presence, extent of tumor · Identify possible metastasis · Evaluate functions of involved and uninvolved body systems · Obtain tissue and cells for analysis, including tumor stage & grade Laboratory tests · Imaging · Biopsy · Endoscopic procedures
:::

:::slides Slide 11 · Surgery
Surgical resection of a tumor – solid tumor malignancies; goal: total excision · Debulking of tumor mass · Redirection of vital functions · Reconstruction
:::

:::slides Slide 12 · External, internal, systemic
Radiation therapy – localized delivery of ionizing radiation, inducing cell death · External – via external beam; CyberKnife · Internal – placement in a cavity (brachytherapy) · Systemic – vascular administration of a radioactive substance
:::

:::slides Slide 13 · Chemo, immunotherapy, targeted, hormonal
Antineoplastic medications · Chemotherapy · Immunological therapy · Targeted agents · Hormonal agents
:::

:::slides Slide 14 · Stem cell & bone marrow transplant
Stem cell and bone marrow transplants · Administration of high-dose antineoplastic therapy · Infusion of stem cells or bone marrow
:::

:::slides Slide 15 · Caring for the oncology patient
All subspecialties of med-surg – oncology; geriatric nursing · Variety of care settings – acute or chronic · Pain control
:::
