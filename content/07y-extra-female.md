### Table 65.3 · medications for breast cancer and female reproductive disorders ★ (exam: medications)

| Class | Examples | Key nursing points |
|---|---|---|
| **SERM** (endocrine) | **Tamoxifen (Nolvadex)** | Blocks estrogen receptor; 5–10 yrs. Common: hot flashes, night sweats, irregular bleeding, vaginal dryness, ↓libido. Rare: DVT/PE, stroke, uterine cancer, cataracts. **Avoid SSRIs** (reduce tamoxifen metabolism) |
| **Aromatase inhibitor** | Anastrozole, exemestane, letrozole | Block androgen → estrogen conversion; 5–10 yrs. Osteoporosis, fractures |
| **Chemotherapy** | Doxorubicin, paclitaxel, docetaxel, 5-FU, cyclophosphamide, carboplatin | Hair loss, N/V, mouth sores, ↓WBC (infection), neuropathy, premature ovarian failure |
| **Immunotherapy** | Pembrolizumab (Keytruda) | Given with chemo before surgery. Fatigue, rash, diarrhea, thyroid problems |
| **Targeted** | Trastuzumab (Herceptin) | For HER2+. Fatigue, joint pain, diarrhea, fever; **cardiotoxicity** |
| **Combined OCPs** | Sprintec, Yaz, patch, NuvaRing | ↑DVT risk. **Avoid with estrogen-positive breast cancer** |
| **Progestin injection** | Depo-Provera | IM every 3 months; weight gain |
| **Levonorgestrel IUD** | Mirena, Kyleena | Bleeding changes; rare perforation |
| **GnRH agonist** | Leuprolide (Lupron) | Hot flashes, ↓bone density |
| **Progestins / estrogens** | Provera / Premarin | **Estrogen without progestin (intact uterus) ↑ endometrial cancer** |

**Case: Karen Sims (estrogen-positive).** Which order to verify? **B. Oral contraceptives** (estrogen could fuel the tumor).
Male breast cancer meds: tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors, fulvestrant only if receptor-positive; trastuzumab/pertuzumab for HER2+.

### Breast cancer: treatment by stage

| Stage | Treatment plan |
|---|---|
| **LCIS** | No treatment; surveillance |
| **DCIS** | Surgery (>4 cm → mastectomy); antiestrogen ↓ recurrence |
| **Early invasive** | Excision → radiation or chemo; endocrine therapy (tamoxifen) if hormone + |
| **Intermediate/advanced (operable)** | Neoadjuvant chemo → surgery → radiation; targeted; endocrine 5–10 yrs |
| **Locally advanced/inoperable** | Neoadjuvant chemo → modified radical mastectomy → post-op chemo + radiation |
| **Recurrence/metastatic** | Mastectomy, excision, radiation, chemo; weigh benefit vs toxicity |

- **Skin changes** include **peau d'orange**. Classic cancerous lump: hard, irregular, painless, immovable (fixed).
- **Modified radical mastectomy** = all breast tissue + axillary nodes; chest muscle stays. **Lumpectomy** = tumor + margin; always followed by radiation (chemo first if indicated).
- **Most common long-term complication = immobility of the shoulder ("frozen shoulder")** (purple note). Radiation effects: red, itchy, irritated skin; patchy desquamation; breast swelling; **pneumonitis (dry cough) 6 weeks–6 months later.**
- **Slide wording:** *Genetic carriers have a 50–80% lifetime risk.* Breast cancer "most commonly impacts soft tissue such as abdomen and lung" (your notes).
- **Epidemiology:** the single risk factor for breast cancer is a woman's age. 2.3 million new cases globally (2022); 321,910 invasive and 60,730 in-situ cases in the US; 2,670 in men.

### Cervical screening guidelines (exam topic) ★

| Age | Screening recommendation |
|---|---|
| **<21** | No screening |
| **21–29** | **Pap every 3 years** (HPV test only reflexively if atypical cells) |
| **25+ (if available)** | Primary HPV screening every 5 years (ACS prefers starting at 25) |
| **30–65** | Primary HPV, or Pap + HPV cotest every 5 years, or Pap alone every 3 years |
| **>65** | May stop if adequate negative screening and no CIN 2 in 20 years |
| **Hysterectomy (benign)** | No more Paps if no history of CIN 2 |
| **High risk** | HIV, immunosuppression, DES exposure, prior dysplasia → more often |

**Case:** a 25-year-old, last Pap at 21, no primary HPV available. Today: **breast exam, pelvic exam, Pap smear (no HPV cotest under 30).**
Diagnosis path: Pap → colposcopy + cervical biopsy (diagnostic) → MRI, PET, CT, CXR. Surgical: radical trachelectomy (early stage, wants fertility) · radical hysterectomy + node dissection (no fertility desire) · pelvic exenteration (recurrence). Medical: chemoradiation (brachytherapy = internal radiation into/next to tumor) · chemotherapy. **HPV vaccine at 11–12, can start at 9.**

### Four female cancers: key fact row
**Breast:** 2nd cancer death in women · **Ovarian:** deadliest gyn · **Uterine:** most common gyn · **Cervical:** most preventable. Typical ages: breast ↑ with age · ovarian 70s · uterine 55–65 · cervical 35–44. Treatment summary: breast surgery ± chemo, radiation, endocrine · ovarian debulking → chemo · uterine hysterectomy ± radiation/chemo · cervical surgery or chemoradiation.

### Ovarian and uterine extras
- **Ovarian:** history of breastfeeding and OC use is protective; progesterone-only HRT. Risk with long-term estrogen/progesterone HRT, advanced age (mostly 70s), BRCA1/2, hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer. Protective = anything that stops ovulation. Surgical: debulking, removal of uterus, ovaries, and tubes.
- **Uterine:** 2.6% lifetime risk in developed nations; 2–5% hereditary. Obesity: fat makes estrogen. **Teach prevention:** obesity and unopposed estrogen are the most common risk factors. Also early menarche/late menopause, PCOS, DM.
